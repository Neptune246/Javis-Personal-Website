    Generalising angular momentum

    Preview of spin concept

    Spin is introduced in the early development of quantum mechanics to refer to the intrinsic angular momentum (like the spin of a planet). This word is kept until now even though an electron does not spin physically.

    For now, spin is actually intrinsic angular momentum of an electron.

    Review of angular momentum

    Angular momentum ladder operators

    The angular momentum ladder operators are similar to that of ladder operators of a and is defined as: $$ L_{\pm} = L_{x} \pm i L_{y}. $$

    \(L_+\) is a step-up operator and can be observed from $$ L^2(L_+f_{\lambda,\mu})=\lambda\hbar^2(L_+f_{\lambda,\mu}) $$ and $$ L_z \left( L_+ f_{\lambda,\mu} \right) = (\mu + 1) \hbar \left( L_+ f_{\lambda,\mu} \right) $$ Note: - The operator only affects \(L_z\) instead of \(L^2\) because \(L^2\) commutes \(L_+\) but \(L_z\) does not commute with \(L_+\) - This is because \(L^2\) depends only on \(l\) while the operator only affect \(m\). The ladder operators rotate the state within the same \(l\)-subspace, altering its orientation but not its total angular momentum magnitude.

    Properties of the ladder operators

    There is a top and bottom rung to this ladder, where at the top and bottom rung, the operator (acting on a function) gives zero

    Overview of general angular momentum

    • \[ [J_x, J_y] = i\hbar J_z, \quad [J_y, J_z] = i\hbar J_x, \quad [J_z, J_x] = i\hbar J_y, $$ $$ [J^2, J_x] = [J^2, J_y] = [J^2, J_z] = 0 \]
    • \[ J^2 f_{j,m_j} = j(j+1) \hbar^2 f_{j,m_j} \quad \text{and} \quad J_z f_{j,m_j} = m_j \hbar f_{j,m_j} \]
    • $$ J_z J_+ f_{j,m_j} = (m_j + 1) \hbar J_+ f_{j,m_j}, \qquad J_z J_- f_{j,m_j} = (m_j - 1) \hbar J_- f_{j,m_j} $$

    So basically the generalised angular momentum is \(J\). Here \(m_j\) is magnetic quantum number and \(j\) is orbital quantum number.
