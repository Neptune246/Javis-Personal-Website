    Eigenfunctions of various potential

    Finite square well

    • Following the same method as that of infinite square well but remember that we need continuity of \(\psi\) and \(d\psi / dx\). This information allows us to find the coefficient for \(x > L/2\) and \(x < -L/2\)
    • To calculate the probability of a particle appearing in a certain section, we need to do the integration in piecewise fashion. $$ \int_{-\infty}^{-L/2} |\psi(x)|^2 dx + \int_{-L/2}^{L/2} |\psi(x)|^2 dx + \int_{L/2}^{\infty} |\psi(x)|^2 dx = 1 $$

    1D harmonic oscillator

    \(V(x) = kx^2/2 = m\omega^2 x^2\) where \(\omega = \sqrt{k/m}\)

    It has wide application because the potential \(V(x)\) can be approximated as $\(V(x) \approx V(x_0) + \cancelto{0}{\left.\frac{dV}{dx}\right|_{x_0}(x-x_0)} + \frac{1}{2}\left.\frac{d^2V}{dx^2}\right|_{x_0}(x-x_0)^2 + \cdots\)$ where the second term cancels because \(dV/dx = F = 0\) at equilibrium position.

    Ladder Operators

    For \(H\psi(x) = E\psi(x)\), the step-up operator is moving up the ladder in energies: $\(H(a_+\psi_n) = (E_n+\hbar\omega)a_+\psi_n\)$ and the step-down operator is moving down the ladder in energies: $\(H(a_-\psi_n) = (E_n-\hbar\omega)a_-\psi_n\)$ However, there is a bottom rung, i.e. \(a_-\psi_0 = 0\) where there is no more to move down.

    Here, the Hamiltonian \(H\) is \(\hat{H} = \hbar\omega \left( \hat{a}_+ \hat{a}_- + \frac{1}{2} \right)\)

    [!note] \(\hat{a}_{\pm} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\hbar m\omega}}(\mp i\hat{p} + m\omega \hat{x})\)

    Properties of harmonic potential

    \(E_n = (n +1/2)\hbar \omega\)

    The energy is non-zero due to Heisenberg uncertainty principle. The system cannot sit motionless at the bottom of its potential well, or else the momentum and position can be determined precisely at the same time.

    Commutator algebra

    \[[A, A] = AA - AA = 0\]
    \[[A+B, C] = [A,C] + [B,C]\]
    \[ [AB, C] = ABC - CAB = A(BC - CB) + (AB - BA)C = A[B,C] + [A,C]B $$ $$ [A, BC] = ABC - BCA = (AB - BA)C + B(AC - CA) = [A,B]C + B[A,C] \]

    Consequences of non-commutation/commutation

    If operators commute, then they share a common set of eigenfunctions, so measuring one does not disturb the wavefunction for the measurement of the next.

    \(\hat{B} |\psi\rangle\) is an eigenstate of \(\hat{A}\) and \(\hat{A}|\psi\rangle\) is an eigenstate of \(\hat{B}\)
