    Fundamental and FT of common functions

    Central Idea of Fourier Optics

    • Decomposing field in initial plane into a sum of plane waves
    • Propagate plane waves downstream into a new plane
    • Sum again with modified phases to obtain diffracted field

    The goal in light: Find link between distribution of propagation angles (angular spectrum) and spatial distribution via Fourier techniques

    Fourier Series

    \[ f(x) = f(x + d) = a_0 + \sum_{j=1}^\infty a_j \cos(2\pi u_j x) + \sum_{j=1}^\infty b_j \sin(2\pi u_j x) $$ where $$ a_0 = \frac{1}{d} \int_{0}^{d} f(x) \, dx \]
    \[ a_j = \frac{2}{d} \int_{0}^{d} f(x) \cos\left(\frac{2\pi j x}{d}\right) \, dx \]
    \[ b_j = \frac{2}{d} \int_{0}^{d} f(x) \sin\left(\frac{2\pi j x}{d}\right) \, dx \]

    and the cos and sin in the coefficients are the basis functions (to show how the function \(f(x)\) modifies the basis functions). We can simplify this by using complex notation:

    $$ f(x) = \sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty} c_j e^{i\frac{2\pi j x}{d}} $$ where $$ c_j = \frac{1}{d} \int_0^d f(x) e^{-\frac{2\pi i j x}{d}} \, dx $$ and the real part of this coefficient is the 'amount' of cos we need and complex part is the 'amount' of sin we need

    Fourier Transform

    • Replace Fourier Series to a continuum
    • It extends to non-periodic functions

    Dirac Delta Function

    \[ \delta(x) = \begin{cases} \infty & x = 0 \\ 0 & x \neq 0 \end{cases} $$ It pick out values of a function $$ \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \delta(x) f(x) \, dx = f(0) \]
    \[ \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \delta(x - x_1) f(x) \, dx = f(x_1) \]

    Intuitively, since the delta function is a vertical, infinite straight line at origin, when multiplied to a function, it gives the value at the origin. If the \(\delta(x)\) is shifted by a finite amount, it gives the value of the function at that position.

    FT of rectangle function

    [!Definition] $$ \operatorname{rect}(x) = \begin{cases} 1 & |x| \leq \frac{1}{2} \ 0 & |x| > \frac{1}{2} \end{cases} $$

    $$ \mathcal{F}\operatorname{rect}(x) = \frac{\sin(\pi u)}{\pi u} = \operatorname{sinc}(\pi u) $$ Normally, we use \(\operatorname{rect}(x/a)\) where \(a\) is the width of the slit.

    FT of cosine function
    \[ \mathcal{F}[\cos(2\pi v_o x)](u) = \frac{1}{2} \left[ \delta(v - v_0) + \delta(v + v_0) \right] \]
    FT of Gauss function

    [!Definition] $$ \operatorname{gauss}\left(\frac{x}{w_0}\right) = \exp\left{-\frac{x^2}{w_0^2}\right} $$

    $\(\mathcal{F}[\operatorname{gauss}(x/w_0)](u) = \sqrt{\pi} w_0 \operatorname{gauss}(\pi u w_0)\)$

    FT of Comb function

    [!Definition] $$ \text{Ш}(x) = \sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty} \delta(x - n) $$

    It gives the same thing:

    \[\mathcal{F}[\operatorname{III}(x)] = \operatorname{III}(u)\]
    FT of Circular function

    [!Definition] $$ \operatorname{circ}(\rho'/D) = \begin{cases} 1 & \rho' \leq D/2 \ 0 & \rho' > D/2 \end{cases} $$ where D is the diameter of the aperture and \(\rho^2 = x'^2 + y'^2\)

    $$ \mathcal{F}\left\operatorname{circ}\left( \frac{\rho}{D} \right)\right = \frac{\pi D^2}{4} \operatorname{jinc}(\pi w D) $$ where \(w^2 = u^2 + v^2\)
