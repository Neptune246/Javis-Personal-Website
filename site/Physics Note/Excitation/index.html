    • Energy of the Excitation
    • Remarks
    • Goldstone's theorem

    Recall from Superfluidity that the broken symmetry potential is a Mexican hat. There are two types of excitation here: 1. Amplitude excitation where it costs the order parameter energy to be excited (from the bottom of the potential to the maximum), creating massive particles. 2. Goldstone mode where it rolls around the gutter (breaking of continuous symmetry because phase changes but not for amplitude excitation where the energy changes instead of the phase) and it takes less energy. It only happens in broken symmetry state with 2D or 3D order parameter case.

    Here we use magnetism to show that when we break the global symmetry (introducing flipped spin), we create an [[Setup/Magnon|excitation]]. We will construct more than one energy state and find the lowest energy state. We will use Heisenberg model where \(D= 3\). We will use different method to derive the [[Setup/Magnon|Magnon]].

    We start from [[Setup/Exchange Interaction and Ferromagnetism|interaction energy]] $$ \hat{H} = -\sum_{\langle i,j \rangle} J \hat{S}i \cdot \hat{S}_j = -J \sum_i \left[ S_i^z S^+) \right] $$ We have a ground state of [[Setup/Exchange Interaction and Ferromagnetism|ferromagnetism]] when }^z + \frac{1}{2} (S_i^+ S_{i+1}^- + S_i^- S_{i+1\(T < T_c\) and all spins are aligned. $\(|\Phi\rangle=|\uparrow\uparrow\uparrow\uparrow\uparrow\uparrow\cdots\rangle\)$where the energy is $$ E = -\frac{NJ}{4} $$ Let's find an excited state. We want to find a correct excited state when the symmetry is broken. We try first by flipping a spin at \(j\) position: \(|j\rangle = |\uparrow\uparrow\uparrow\downarrow\uparrow\uparrow\uparrow\cdots\rangle\). We apply the Hamiltonian to this state $$ \hat{H}|j\rangle = \left(-\frac{NJ}{4} + J\right)|j\rangle - \frac{J}{2}|j+1\rangle - \frac{J}{2}|j-1\rangle $$ To future me: you might wonder how \(j-1\) araised. Note that index \(i\) is arbitrary. For the \(|j-1\rangle\) part, we define \(i = j-1\). Back to the topic, we find that \(|j\rangle\) is not an eigenstate of \(H\). We then try introducing spin waves by defining a new state $\(|q\rangle = \frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_j e^{iqja}|j\rangle\)$ where \(q\) is the wave vector and \(a\) is the lattice spacing. We will get $\(\hat{H}|q\rangle = \hat{H} \frac{1}{\sqrt{N}} \sum_j e^{iq j a} |j\rangle = \left( -\frac{NJ}{4} + \underbrace{J (1 - \cos q a)}_\text{energy cost} \right) |q\rangle\)$ which is an eigenstate of \(H\).

    Energy of the Excitation

    We can find the energy cost using dispersion relation $\(\hbar\omega(q) = J(1 - \cos(qa))\)$ And we can find that \(q \rightarrow 0\), \(\omega \rightarrow 0\), meaning that if we have large wave vector, we have large wavelength and the excitation costs basically no energy.

    For small \(q\), we have the dispersion relation \(\omega \approx q^2\), from there we can get the [[Setup/Density of States, Expectation Values and DeBroglie Thermal Wavelength|density of state]] \(g(q) \propto q^2 dq\) and the energy \(g(\omega) d\omega \approx \omega^{1/2} d\omega\) just like phonon.

    Goldstone's theorem

    • Whenever a continuous symmetry is broken, we get a new massless excitation i.e. a pseudo-particle.
