    PN Junction Equilibrium

    Now we have p-type and n-type semiconductors and we could make a diode by combining them. Here we take a close look at the depletion region i.e. the pn junction

    Recall that the donor concentration is \(n\sim N_D\) and \(p = n_i^2/N_D\) for the donor dopant and it's the other way round for the acceptor dopant. Here we ignore the electron in CB and holes in VB and focus only on the ionised donors and acceptors.

    Electric field

    Gauss's law says $\(\vec{\nabla} \cdot \vec{\mathcal{E}} = \frac{d\mathcal{E}}{dx} = \frac{\rho(x)}{\epsilon_r \epsilon_0}\)$ where \(\rho(x)\) is the charge density and we care about the \(x\) axis. Given that the depletion region with length from \(-w_p\) to \(w_n\), has no charge so we have $\(\rho(x) = \begin{cases} -eN_A & -w_p < x < 0 \\ eN_D & 0 < x < w_n \\ 0 & \text{elsewhere} \end{cases}\)$ where for the region \(-w_p < x < 0\), the depletion zone is negative because the electron from the n-Si flows to that region; for the region \(0 < x < w_n\), the region is positive because the holes from p-Si flows to that region.

    Through integration, we get the electric field $\(\mathcal{E}(x) = \begin{cases} -\frac{eN_A}{\epsilon_r \epsilon_0}(x + w_p), & -w_p < x < 0 \\ \frac{eN_D}{\epsilon_r \epsilon_0}(x - w_n), & 0 < x < w_n \end{cases}\)$ Solving the boundary condition (where we use continuity of electric field at the boundary) gives \(N_Aw_p = N_Dw_D\)

    Electric potential

    The potential is defined as $\(\vec{\mathcal{E}} = -\vec{\nabla}\phi \quad (\phi = \text{electrostatic potential})\)$

    By more integration, we get $\(\phi(x) = \begin{cases} \frac{eN_A}{2\epsilon_r\epsilon_0}(x + w_p)^2 & -w_p < x < 0 \\ \phi_{bi} - \frac{eN_D}{2\epsilon_r\epsilon_0}(x - w_n)^2 & 0 < x < w_n \end{cases}\)$ where \(\phi_{bi}\) is the build-in potential (potential difference) and it is the solution of the boundary condition.

    Energy level diagram for pn-junction

    Before contacting,

    After contact the energy level \(E_c\) and \(E_v\) follows the electron energy lineshape

    From here we can calculate \(\phi_{bi} = (\mu_n - \mu_p)/e\) where $\(\begin{aligned} \mu_n &= E_c - kT \ln \left( \frac{N_c}{N_D} \right) \\ \mu_p &= E_v + kT \ln \left( \frac{N_v}{N_A} \right) \end{aligned}\)$ as can be derived from the results in Statistical Mechanics for Semiconductors and Intrinsic Semiconductors and Extrinsic Semiconductors. We get $$ \phi_{bi} = \frac{kT}{e} \ln \left( \frac{N_A N_D}{n_i^2} \right)$$ and by considering the potential is continuous at \(x=0\), we get $\(\begin{aligned} w_n &= \left[ \frac{2\epsilon_r \epsilon_0 \phi_{bi}}{e} \left( \frac{N_A}{N_D} \right) \left( \frac{1}{N_A + N_D} \right) \right]^{1/2} \\ w_p &= \left[ \frac{2\epsilon_r \epsilon_0 \phi_{bi}}{e} \left( \frac{N_D}{N_A} \right) \left( \frac{1}{N_A + N_D} \right) \right]^{1/2} \end{aligned}\)$
