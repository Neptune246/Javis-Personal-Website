    Metal II

    Density of states

    Similar to the density of states we mentioned previously, for a Fermi sphere, the number of states is $\(N(k) = \frac{\frac{4}{3}\pi k^3}{(\frac{2\pi}{L})^3} \times 2 = \frac{Vk^3}{3\pi^2}\)$ The 2 is because of two spin states. Given that the energy is \(E = \hbar^2k^2/2m\), the number of states in term of energy is $$ N(E) = \frac{V}{3\pi^2} \left( \frac{2mE}{\hbar^2} \right)^{\frac{3}{2}} $$ and the density of state is the derivative of \(N\) with respect to energy.

    Total number of electron, \(N_e\) and total energy

    This is slightly different

    \[N_e = \int^\infty _ 0 \frac{1}{e^{E-\mu/k_BT}+1} g(E)dE\]

    The \(\mu\) is whatever number that can be used for this value to be true. The total energy is then (setting the upper bound of the integral to \(E_F\) and solve the integral) $\(E_F = \left(3\pi^2\frac{Ne}{V}\right)^{2/3}\frac{\hbar^2}{2m} = \frac{\hbar^2k_F^2}{2m}\)$ and from there you can find \(k_F\) (we use \(n\) to replace \(N_e/V\))

    Properties

    Fermi temperature (not the real temperature) $\(E_F = k_BT_F\)$

    Fermi energy is a chemical potential at temperature \(T=0\) and you can (not in all cases) call it the energy level of the most occupied state.

    Volume of Fermi sphere

    $\(V_F = \frac{4}{3}\pi k_F^3 = \frac{4}{3}\pi (3\pi^2n) = \frac{4\pi^3}{a^3}\)$ where \(n = 1/a^3\) i.e. \(N_e = 1\)

    In reciprocal space (square), $\(V_{BZ} = \left(\frac{2\pi}{a}\right)^3 = \frac{8\pi^3}{a^3}\)$
