    Meson

    The state notation for a particle is $\(n^{2s+1} \ L_J\)$ where \(n\) is the principle quantum number, \(s\) is the spin, \(L\) is the orbital angular momentum and \(J\) is the total angular momentum just like atomic physics notation

    The excitation of meson can occur in many ways, e.g. increase in \(L, n\) and spin flip

    where the individual spin is \(s =1\) when spins are aligned, otherwise \(s=0\).

    Parity of Meson

    The parity of the meson can be evaluated as $\(P(\text{meson}) = P(q)P(\bar{q})\underbrace{(-1)^L}_{\text{orbital contribution}} = (-1)(-1)^L\)$

    Note that the parity will always be opposite in a quark and antiquark i.e. one of them must be opposite to another. The contribution of the orbital quantum number is from the symmetricity of the spherical harmonics: even \(L\) symmetrical, odd \(L\) antisymmetric.

    Excitation

    How much energy should we input to excite a meson? Let's take the spin flip case as an example. The potential for strong interaction $$ V_{\text{spin-spin}}(q \bar{q}) = \frac{32\pi}{9} \left( \mathbf{S}1 \cdot \mathbf{S}_2 \right) \ \alpha_s \ \delta(x) $$ and it gives $$ \begin{aligned} E = \langle \psi | V | \psi \rangle \[1.5em]}

    = \frac{32\pi}{9} \alpha_s \frac{S_q \cdot S_\bar{q}}{M_q M_\bar{q}} \psi(0) \end{aligned} $$

    Therefore the interaction between spins is $\(\Delta E \propto \mathbf{S}_1 \cdot \mathbf{S}_2\)$Just remember from spins in magnetism course. To evaluate this, we find the total spin of a meson $$ \langle S^2 \rangle = \langle (S_q + S_{\bar{q}})^2 \rangle = \langle S_q^2 \rangle + \underbrace{\langle 2 \mathbf{S}q \cdot \mathbf{S}}} \rangle\text{what we want} + \langle S^2 \rangle $$ Using the fact that }\(\hat{S^2} = S(S+1)\hbar\) and for fermions, \(s= 1/2\) we have $$ \langle \hat{S}^2 \rangle = \frac{1}{2} \left( \frac{1}{2} + 1 \right) + \frac{1}{2} \left( \frac{1}{2} + 2 \right) + 2 \langle \hat{S}q \cdot \hat{S}_q \rangle $$ Now remember that: individual spin is \(s =1\) when spins are aligned, otherwise \(s=0\) so we have $$ \begin{pmatrix} 0 \ 2 \end{pmatrix} = \frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{1}{2}+1\right) + \frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{1}{2}+1\right) + 2\langle \hat{\mathbf{S}}_q \cdot \hat{\mathbf{S}} \rangle $$}

    and it gives $$ \langle \hat{S}q \cdot \hat{S} \rangle = \begin{cases} -\frac{3}{4} & , \quad \langle S^2 \rangle = 0 \quad \text{(Spin misaligned)} \ \[0.5em] +\frac{1}{4} & , \quad \langle S^2 \rangle = 2 \quad \text{(Spin aligned)} \end{cases} $$}

    PS: when evaluating the wave function and you are trying to work from a symmetric to anti-symmetric wave function, just remember it is symmetric times an anti-symmetric component.
