    Ginzburg Landau Theory For Superconductivity

    There are currently two theories for superconductivity: 1. BCS theory (microscopic theory of superconductivity) 2. Ginzburg-Landau Theory (Phenomenological/macroscopic theory of superconductivity)

    GL postulated that something will change from normal metallic state to superconducting state and it is called the order parameter. The order parameter is either non-zero or 0 depending on the critical temperature $\(\psi(T) = \begin{cases} \psi(T)\neq 0 & T < T_c \\ 0 & T > T_c \end{cases}\)$ They also postulated that order parameter is a complex number and the real-valued free energy is a smooth function of order parameter. So if we Taylor expand the free energy in terms of the order parameter, the only possible case is $\(G_s(T) = G_N(T) + a(T) |\psi|^2+\frac{b(T)}{2}|\psi|^4\)$

    where \(a(T)\) and \(b(T)\) are coefficients that depend on \(T\). This is because even power terms can cancel the imaginary part of \(\psi\). In superconductivity, the order parameter is the cooper pairs where \(|\psi|^2 = n_s/2\) where \(n_s\) is the density of superconducting electrons.

    In normal state, \(a(T) > 0\) where it is a parabolic shape

    But in superconducting state, \(a(T) < 0\)

    In both cases, the coefficient \(b(T) >0\) to make sure the curves have a minimum. To simplify the equation, we take the Taylor expansion of the coefficients. Since the coefficients are arbitrary, we can define the order of expansion such that it simplifies the equations: $\(\begin{aligned} a(T) &\approx \dot{a}(T - T_c) + \dots \quad (\dot{a} > 0) \\ b(T) &\approx b + \dots \quad (b > 0) \end{aligned}\)$

    where the first order of \(a(T)\) is zero and only keep the first order of \(b(T)\). The stable point is \(\Delta G = \dot{a}(T-T_c) + b|\psi|^2 =0 \implies\) $\(|\psi|^2 = \begin{cases} \frac{\dot{a}(T_c - T)}{b} & T < T_c \\ 0 & T > T_c \end{cases}\)$

    We eventually get $\(\begin{aligned} G_n(T) - G_s(T) &= -a(T)|\psi|^2 - \frac{b(T)}{2}|\psi|^4 \\ &= \frac{[\dot{a}(T - T_c)]^2}{2b} \quad \text{(Condensation energy)} \end{aligned}\)$

    Recall from Meissner Effect that $\(G_N[0] - G_s[0] = \frac{B_c(T)^2}{2\mu_0}\)$

    We can eventually work out \(B_c(T)\) in terms of temperature and the entropy change which is \(S = - (dG/dT)\) to see that there is no latent heat/entropy change at \(T_c\) i.e. second order transition.

    Inhomogeneous Systems

    Order parameters can depends on the position \(\mathbf{r}\) if the surfaces/systems are inhomogeneous $\(G_s(T, \mathbf{r}) = G_N(T, \mathbf{r}) + \frac{\hbar^2}{2m} |\nabla \psi(\mathbf{r})|^2 + a(T) |\psi(\mathbf{r})|^2 + \frac{b(T)}{2} |\psi(\mathbf{r})|^4\)$ where the \(m\) term is the energy associated with the gradient of the order parameter and it is readily reduce to the homogeneous case by setting it to constant \(\psi(\mathbf{r}) \rightarrow \psi\) . Since a system will have the tendency to stay in its lowest energy, we minimise the energy $\(G_s(T) = G_N(T) + \int \left( \frac{\hbar^2}{2m} |\nabla \psi(\mathbf{r})|^2 + a(T) |\psi(\mathbf{r})|^2 + \frac{b(T)}{2} |\psi(\mathbf{r})|^4 \right) d\mathbf{r}\)$ and we get the nonlinear Schrodinger equation: $\(\Rightarrow \frac{\hbar^2}{2m} \nabla^2 \psi(\mathbf{r}) + \left[ a(T) + \frac{b(T)}{2} |\psi(\mathbf{r})|^2 \right] \psi(\mathbf{r}) = 0\)$

    By solving this we get a solution $\(\psi(x) = \psi_0 \tanh \left( \frac{x}{\sqrt{2} \xi(T)} \right)\)$ where $\(\xi(T) = \left( \frac{\hbar^2}{2m |a(T)|} \right)^{1/2}\)$ is the coherence length. Physically, consider an interface between the normal metal and superconducting states. The order parameter \(\psi\) has to be zero before \(x = 0\) (normal metal state) and non-zero after. To stay continuous, it has to be like this described by the tanh equation.

    This coherence length is the separation of the Cooper pair electrons.

    Using this knowledge with energy we can differentiate type I or II superconductors. Here we consider two energies:

    1. Magnetic energy
    2. Condensation energy

    When we plot the order parameter graph, we get the coherence length where cooper pair starts to form. The formation of cooper pair will take up the free energy of the system

    In a system, these two energies are in competition.

    Case I: \(\lambda_L \ll \xi\)

    The interfacial energy is high (the interception point). That means this system is not favourable for an interface to form: i.e. no metal and superconductor interface \(\implies\) type I

    Case II: \(\xi \ll \lambda_L\)

    The interfacial energy is low and it is favourable for an interface to form \(\implies\) type II
