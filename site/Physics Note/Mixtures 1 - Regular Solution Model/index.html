    Mixtures 1 Regular Solution Model

    So far we have seen the application of Gibbs free energy in particles at the interface and nucleation. And we have been dealing with various platforms, solids, liquids and gas.

    Here, we take a closer look at the mixing between liquids and we want to predict if two liquids mix or not.

    Reminder and terminology

    Recall that $$ \Delta G_{mix} = G_{mix} - G_{unmix} = \Delta H_{mix} - T\Delta S_{mix} $$ where \(\Delta G_{mix} < 0\) means mixing and \(\Delta G_{mix} > 0\) means demixing.

    Throughout the note, we will use 1. \(N_A\) and \(N_B\) are the number of molecules A and B respectively. 2. \(\mu = \bar{G} = G/N\) as chemical potential or the Gibbs free energy per molecules 3. \(\phi_A = N_A/N, \phi_B = N_B/N\) where \(N=N_A + N_B\) as the fraction of molecules A and B, respectively.

    Entropy term

    Recall from Statistical physics that $$ \bar{S} = -k_B \sum_i \phi_i \ln(\phi_i) $$ $$\implies \bar{S} = -k_B [\phi_A \ln(\phi_A) + \phi_B \ln(\phi_B)] $$ That implies the Gibbs free energy is lowered

    Enthalpy term

    If you take some molecules A and put into B, you need energy to break the bond between them. When you put A into a pile and B into another pile, you form new bonds between A and B. That change of energy affects the internal energy of the system.

    \[ \Delta \bar{U}_{mix} = \bar{U}_{mixed} - \bar{U}_{unmixed} = \begin{pmatrix} \text{energy change per} \\ A - B \text{ bond relative} \\ \text{to } A - A \text{ or } B - B \end{pmatrix} (\# A - B \text{ contacts}) \]

    The probability of A-B contact is given by \(P \sim Z\phi_A \phi_B\) therefore

    $$ \Delta \bar{U}_{mix} = \kappa \phi_A \phi_B $$ where \(\kappa\) is interaction parameter (J/molecule). - If \(\kappa > 0\) disfavour mixing (because it increases free energy) - If \(\kappa<0\) favour mixing

    By definition $$ H \equiv U + PV $$ $\(\implies \Delta \bar{H}_{mix} = \Delta \bar{U}_{mix} + P \underbrace{\Delta V_{mix}}_{\sim 0 (Z = cte)} + V \Delta \underbrace{P_{mix}}_{=0 (P=cte)} = \kappa\phi_A\phi_B\)$

    Regular solution model

    Based on the thermodynamical model above, we have $$ \Delta \bar{G}{mix} = \Delta \bar{H} = \kappa \, \phi_A \phi_B + k_B T [\phi_A \ln(\phi_A) + \phi_B \ln(\phi_B)] $$ i.e. } - T \Delta \bar{S}_{mix\(\Delta H\) increases energy of the system but entropy always decreases the energy.

    Remember that for mixing, \(\Delta G <0\)

    • In almost all cases \(\kappa > 0\) we have two minima and a maximum at \(\phi = \phi_B = 1-\phi_A = 1/2\) because it disfavour mixing. At \(\phi = 1/2\) it is a stable point, from the formula.
