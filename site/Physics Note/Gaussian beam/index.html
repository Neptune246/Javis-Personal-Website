    \[ E^{(0)}=E_{0}e^{-x^{\prime 2}/\omega_{0}^{2}}e^{-y^{\prime 2}/\omega_{0}^{2}} $$ and the aperture function is $$ f\left(x^{\prime},y^{\prime}\right)=e^{-x^{\prime 2}/\omega_{0}^{2}}e^{-y^{\prime 2}/\omega_{0}^{2}} \]

    The Fourier transform of the aperture function is $$ \begin{align} \mathcal{F}\left[f\left(x^{\prime},y^{\prime}\right)\right] &=\iint_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-x^{\prime 2}/\omega_{0}^{2}}e^{-y^{\prime 2}/\omega_{0}^{2}}e^{-i2\pi(ux^{\prime}+vy^{\prime})}dx^{\prime}dy^{\prime} \ &=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-x^{\prime 2}/\omega_{0}^{2}}e^{-i2\pi ux^{\prime}}dx^{\prime}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-y^{\prime 2}/\omega_{0}^{2}}e^{-i2\pi vy^{\prime}}dy^{\prime} \end{align} $$ The integrals are the same, the \(x^\prime\) integral is $$ \begin{align} \int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-x^{\prime 2}/\omega_{0}^{2}}e^{-i2\pi ux^{\prime}}dx^{\prime} &=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-(x^{\prime 2}/\omega_{0}^{2}+i2\pi ux^{\prime})}dx^{\prime} \ &=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-(x^{\prime 2}/\omega_{0}^{2}+i2\pi ux^{\prime}-\pi^{2}u^{2}\omega_{0}^{2})-\pi^{2}u^{2}\omega_{0}^{2}}dx^{\prime} \ &=e^{-\pi^{2}u^{2}\omega_{0}^{2}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-(x^{\prime}/\omega_{0}+i\pi u\omega_{0})^{2}}dx^{\prime} \ &=\omega_{0}e^{-\pi^{2}u^{2}\omega_{0}^{2}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-(x^{\prime}/\omega_{0}+i\pi u\omega_{0})^{2}}d(x^{\prime}/\omega_{0}+i\pi u\omega_{0}) \ &=\omega_{0}e^{-\pi^{2}u^{2}\omega_{0}^{2}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-t^{2}}dt \ &=\omega_{0}e^{-\pi^{2}u^{2}\omega_{0}^{2}}\sqrt{\pi} \end{align} $$

    Hence the final fourier transform is $$ \mathcal{F}\left[f\left(x^{\prime},y^{\prime}\right)\right]=\pi\omega_{0}^{2}e^{-\pi^{2}(u^{2}+v^{2})\omega_{0}^{2}}=\pi\omega_{0}^{2}e^{-\pi^{2}\omega_{0}^{2}(x^{2}+y^{2})/(\lambda^{2}z^{2})}=\pi\omega_{0}^{2}e^{-\pi^{2}\omega_{0}^{2}\rho^{2}/(\lambda^{2}z^{2})} $$

    \[ I^{(z)}=\frac{I_{0}}{\lambda^{2}z^{2}}\pi^{2}\omega_{0}^{4}e^{-2\pi^{2}\omega_{0}^{2}\rho^{2}/(\lambda^{2}z^{2})}=I_{0}\frac{\pi^{2}\omega_{0}^{4}}{\lambda^{2}z^{2}}e^{-2\rho^{2}/\omega^{2}} \]

    where \(\omega = \lambda z/\pi \omega_0\) is the far-field beam radius.

    The angular spread of the beam is $$ \Delta\theta=\frac{\omega}{z}=\frac{\lambda}{\pi\omega_{0}} \quad \text{(92)} $$

    As for the case of a single slit, the Fraunhofer approximation is valid only when the width of the light distribution is much larger than the initial size. The cross over beywee initial size dominating and diffraction dominating occurs at the Rayleigh range, \(z_R = \omega_0/\Delta \theta\) which gives $$ z_{R}=\frac{\pi\omega_{0}^{2}}{\lambda} \quad \text{(93)} $$ It basically means that the beam travels at a distance of \(z_R\) in parallel (collimated) with minimal spread and starts to spread after \(z_R\).

    Rayleigh range is greater than Rayleigh distance due to Gaussian function. The far field condition for Gaussian beams like laser beams is \(z\gg z_R\).
