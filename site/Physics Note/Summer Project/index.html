    Condensed Matter Physics Project details (with reference to Microwave-optical coupling via Rydberg excitons in cuprous oxide by Liam)

    • Purpose: Quantum computers operate at a superconducting temperature (get rid of thermal noises since precise control over qubits is necessary) at a microwave frequency. However it is not feasible for quantum information to be transferred over a long distance, so it is necessary to convert microwave to optical (since we have the technology). The aim of this project is to create a microwave-optical converter.

    • Conversion using mechanical oscillators, nonlinear crystals and Rydberg atoms have been demonstrated but Rydberg atoms (electrons of atoms getting excited to a very high energy level, resulting in high quantum number, an unusual large atomic size and sensitive to external fields) show promises because of large dipole moment associated with transitions between Rydberg states of opposite parity (scaling \(n\) to \(n^2\)).

    • Finding:

      • Microwave field changed the absorption lineshape
      • Sidebands at the microwave frequency added to the coherent second harmonic
      • Complex dependence on principle quantum number and angular momentum
    • Details:
      • Introduction section
        • Rydberg atoms have to be held in place after they are produced. It involves laser cooling to reduce the temperature. But laser cooling produces lower temperature than millikelvin dilution refrigerator. Instead, excitons in cuprous oxide are used because it is already locked in lattice. We can just put it in dilution refrigerator.
        • The choice of cuprous oxide is because for low quantum number, the energy separation scales \(n\) to \(n^{-3}\). Combining with reduced Rydberg constant of excitons, the scaling means that the yellow series of cuprous oxide is accessible to microwave frequency.
        • Observe the coupling between electric field of microwave circuit (studied using one photon absorption spectroscopy and second generation spectroscopy) and the optical properties of cuprous oxide.

    Personal Statement

    I am a second-year undergraduate studying BSc Physics with a strong interest in Condensed Matter Physics (CMP) and Astronomy. I am eager to undertake a summer project to gain valuable research experience and further my academic and career development.

    Having read the CMP project description, I was reminded of an article written by Liam on microwave-optical coupling via Rydberg excitons in cuprous oxide. I was particularly impressed by how the research team used one-photon absorption and second harmonic generation spectroscopy to study the effects of a microwave circuit's electric field on cuprous oxide's optical properties. I am keen to learn more and contribute to this project, whether through experimental investigations of microwave field effects on photoluminescence intensity or data analysis. In the meantime, I am looking forward to apply what I have learnt from my level 2 CMP and lab skills modules to solve a real-world problem.

    My Research-Led Investigation (RLI) focused on identifying non-Keplerian influences in determining Jupiter’s mass using Kepler’s third law. This project provided hands-on experience with telescope operation, the Linux operating system, and image processing tools such as Gaia and DS9. It also strengthened my ability to analyse errors critically and conduct independent research. Furthermore, my Stars and Galaxies module deepened my understanding of astronomical instrumentation and stellar dynamics.

    This project presents an excellent opportunity to expand my knowledge and gain hands-on research experience, and I am excited about the prospect of contributing meaningfully to the team.
