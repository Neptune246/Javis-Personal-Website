    Magnetic Moment and Spin

    Classical

    We start this topic by investigating the Larmor precession, dont know why it is here as well but yes we will start. It is mentioned in Fundamental Equations for Magnetism so here we will quote the results.

    we have a magnetic moment \(\vec{{\mu}}\) which is proportional to the spin \(S\) $\(\vec{\mu} = \gamma S\)$ where \(\gamma\) is the gyroscopic ratio. For electron, we have $\(\mu = g_s \gamma S = g_s \mu_B S/ \hbar\)$ and because of the torque from the magnetic moment itself, it causes the magnetic moment to precess around the direction of magnetic field.

    Quantum

    The Hamiltonian of the system is given by $$ H = -\vec{\mu} \cdot \vec{B} = -\gamma \mathbf{S} \cdot B\hat{z} = -\gamma B S_z = -\gamma B \frac{\hbar}{2} \sigma_z = \frac{\hbar \omega_L}{2} \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \ 0 & -1 \end{pmatrix} $$ where the Hamiltonian has only one potential term \(E = -\vec{\mu} \cdot \vec{B}\). Solve this Hamiltonian using eigenvalue problems with the spin states as the eigenvectors with their respective eigen-energies gives $$ H \begin{pmatrix} 1 \ 0 \end{pmatrix} = \frac{\hbar \omega_L}{2} \begin{pmatrix} 1 \ 0 \end{pmatrix}, \quad H \begin{pmatrix} 0 \ 1 \end{pmatrix} = -\frac{\hbar \omega_L}{2} \begin{pmatrix} 0 \ 1 \end{pmatrix} $$

    At time \(t=0\), the state is in superposition of spin-up and -down, $$ \chi(t=0) = \begin{pmatrix} a \ b \end{pmatrix} = a \begin{pmatrix} 1 \ 0 \end{pmatrix} + b \begin{pmatrix} 0 \ 1 \end{pmatrix} = \cos\left(\frac{\alpha}{2}\right) \begin{pmatrix} 1 \ 0 \end{pmatrix} + \sin\left(\frac{\alpha}{2}\right) \begin{pmatrix} 0 \ 1 \end{pmatrix} $$ where the coefficients are expressed in terms of angles to visualise the spinning of the magnetic moment. This is converted to the time-dependent: $$ \chi(t) = \begin{pmatrix} \cos\left(\frac{\alpha}{2}\right) e^{-i\frac{\omega_L t}{2}} \ \sin\left(\frac{\alpha}{2}\right) e^{+i\frac{\omega_L t}{2}} \end{pmatrix} $$

    Here, we can calculate the expectation value of the spin operator under the influence of magnetic field $\(\langle \chi(t) | S | \chi(t) \rangle = \langle \chi(t) | S_x + S_y +S_z | \chi(t) \rangle\)$ where we can solve each component individually using Pauli Matrices and the spin states. The results $$ = \frac{\hbar}{2} \sin(\alpha) \cdot [\cos(\omega_L t) \, \hat{x} + \sin(\omega_L t) \, \hat{y}] + \frac{\hbar}{2} \cos(\alpha) \, \hat{z} $$ and the magnetic moment is precessing at a frequency of \(\omega_L = E / \hbar = \mu_B B/\hbar = \gamma B\)

    Hilbert Space and Total Spins of two spin-1/2 particles in a system

    Consider two spin-1/2 particles in a system. Each one of them have their individual Hilbert space with their individual spins. In this case the total Hamiltonian is the tensor product of their Hamiltonian.

    \[ H_{\text{total}} = H_1 \otimes H_2 \]

    The spin for each particles are $$ S_{jz} \begin{pmatrix} 1 \ 0 \end{pmatrix}^{(j)} = \frac{\hbar}{2} \begin{pmatrix} 1 \ 0 \end{pmatrix}^{(j)}, \quad S_{jz} \begin{pmatrix} 0 \ 1 \end{pmatrix}^{(j)} = -\frac{\hbar}{2} \begin{pmatrix} 0 \ 1 \end{pmatrix}^{(j)}, \quad j = 1, 2 $$ where \(j\) is the index for the particles. We simplify the notation by $$ \alpha(j) = \begin{pmatrix} 1 \ 0 \end{pmatrix}^{(j)}, \quad \beta(j) = \begin{pmatrix} 0 \ 1 \end{pmatrix}^{(j)} $$ therefore it gives $$ S_{jz} \alpha(j) = \frac{\hbar}{2} \alpha(j), \quad S_{jz} \beta(j) = -\frac{\hbar}{2} \beta(j), \quad j = 1, 2 $$ These form the basis states for the two-particle system in this box $$ \alpha(1) \alpha(2), \quad \alpha(1) \beta(2), \quad \beta(1) \alpha(2), \quad \beta(1) \beta(2) $$

    \(S_z\) and the magnetic quantum number

    The spin operator to the basis states, $\(S_z \alpha(1)\alpha(2)=(S_{1z} + S_{2z})\alpha(1)\alpha(2)\)$ we can get the magnetic quantum number of the system.

    [!note] $\(\alpha(1)\alpha(2) \quad |S = 1, M = 1\rangle\)$ $\(\alpha(1)\beta(2) \quad |S = ?, M = 0\rangle\)$ $\(\beta(1)\alpha(2) \quad |S = ?, M = 0\rangle\)$ $\(\beta(1)\beta(2) \quad |S = 1, M = -1\rangle\)$

    \(S^2\) and \(S(S+1)\) eigenvalues

    Instead of directly adding \(S_{1z} + S_{2z}\) we have \(S^2 = (S_1+S_2)^2\) where \(S = S_x +S_y +S_z\)

    The results are stated above. For the question marks ones, the eigenvalues are not in the eigenstates. In fact, they are in superposition i.e. indistinguishable. We can distinguish two possibility based on symmetry:

    1. Symmetric combination
    2. Antisymmetric combination

    These two combinations could be a result in \(S\)

    [!note] $\(\alpha(1)\alpha(2) \quad \rightarrow |S = 1, M = 1\rangle\)$ $\(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} (\alpha(1)\beta(2) + \beta(1)\alpha(2)) \quad \rightarrow |S = 1, M = 0\rangle\)$ $\(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} (\alpha(1)\beta(2) - \beta(1)\alpha(2)) \quad \rightarrow |S = 0, M = 0\rangle\)$ $\(\beta(1)\beta(2) \quad \rightarrow |S = 1, M = -1\rangle\)$

    Note that the total spin in the box is either 0 or 1. For \(S=1\) states, the particles are symmetric under exchange i.e., it will be indistinguishable and you don't know if they are swapped. They have different values of \(M\) which have different projection of the total spin along the \(z\)-axis

    These three \(S=1\) states are called triplet states while the one \(S=0\) state is called the singlet state.

    Another approach

    The other approach to express the basic states in terms of \(|S, M\rangle\) is to use the raising/lowering operators: $\(S_\pm = S_{1\pm} + S_{2\pm}\)$ where they raise/lower the magnetic quantum number (for the spin case, it increases/decreases the spin by one unit i.e. decrease \(\alpha\) to \(\beta\)). We can start from \(\alpha(1)\alpha(2)\) $$ S_- \alpha(1) \alpha(2) = (S_1^- + S_2^-) \alpha(1) \alpha(2) = (S_1^- \alpha(1)) \alpha(2) + \alpha(1)(S_2^- \alpha(2)) = \hbar \underbrace{(\beta(1)\alpha(2) + \alpha(1)\beta(2))}_{\propto |1,0\rangle} $$ where it can be shown that $\(|1,0\rangle = \frac{\alpha(1)\beta(2) + \beta(1)\alpha(2)}{\sqrt{2}}\)$

    We can do the same for the other combinations. For the \(|0,0\rangle\) state, it will be orthogonal to the triplet states. And it can be proven that it is in the eigenstate of total spin in the box \(S_z\).
