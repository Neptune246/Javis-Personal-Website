    Colliods II Double layer forces and the DLVO theory

    Double layer forces

    • Occur when charged solids are immersed into a liquid that contain ions.
    • When the solids are immersed into the liquid, the ionisation of the surface group or adsorption causes the surface charge imbalance.
    • To maintain the neutrality, the ions from the liquids adsorb to the surface, creating an ionic concentration gradient
    • This gradient could repel (most cases)/attract another solid in a similar condition

    We try to quantify the potential and ionic density near the interface. For some reason without derivation, the ions in the presence of an electrical potential \(\psi\) has the chemical potential of $$ \mu_{\text{ion},i} = \mu_{0,i} + k_B T \ln(\rho_i) + z_i e \psi $$ where \(\rho_i\) is the concentration of ions of type \(i\) (per volume) and \(z_i\) is the valency of ions (e.g. for Ca\(^{++}\) is +2 ) .

    At equilibrium \(\mu_{\text{ion},i} = cte\), we rearrange for \(\rho_i\) and this is the Boltzmann equation that describes the ionic concentration profile of the counter-ions $$ n_i = \underbrace{e^\frac{\mu_{\text{ion},i} - \mu_{0,i}}{k_B T}}{n_0} e^{-\frac{z_i e \psi}{k_B T}} $$ We also have consider the potential screening effect from the ions i.e. potential is related to the distribution of net charge (in other words, both influence each other). This is Poisson equation$$ \underbrace{ze\rho(x)}\text{total charge at \(x\)} = -\varepsilon \varepsilon_0 \frac{d^2\psi(x)}{dx^2} $$where we consider only a dimension. Combining them gives $$ \frac{d^2\psi(x)}{dx^2} = -\frac{z_i e \rho(x)}{\varepsilon \varepsilon_0} = -\frac{z_i e \rho_0}{\varepsilon \varepsilon_0} e^{-\frac{z_i e \psi(x)}{k_B T}} $$ (Boltzmann-Poisson equation) - This is the general case

    Example on a solid in an electrolyte of NaCl

    Debye screening length is \(1/\kappa\) where $$ \kappa = \sqrt{\frac{\sum_i \rho_{0,i} z_i^2 e^2}{\varepsilon \varepsilon_0 k_B T}} $$ is the screening effect in the electrolyte. Beyond this length, the direct electrostatic interaction between two charged objects rapidly falls to zero. Since potential is related to force, the double layer force decays exponentially with the Debye length.

    \(\psi = \psi_0 e^{-\kappa x}\)

    DLVO theory

    • Nothing to say, it is just a balance of the repulsion force (double layer force) and the attraction force (van der Waals) to ensure the stability of colloids

    • Stable colloidal solutions are clear and have no sediments or aggregation
