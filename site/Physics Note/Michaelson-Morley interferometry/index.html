    Michaelson Morley interferometry

    No matter you use plane or spherical waves, they end up the same.

    The beam splitter splits the intensity of the light rays into 50:50, i.e. \(I_1^{(0)} = I_2^{(0)} = I_0^{(0)}/2\) and recalling that \(I = (1/2) \epsilon_0 c E^2\), we get that the fields after the beamsplitter is $$ E_1^{(0)} = E_2^{(0)} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} E^{(0)} $$ and we neglect the reflection of the light ray. After splitting, the wavevectors become $$ \begin{align} \mathbf{k}_1 &= (0, k, 0), \ \mathbf{k}_2 &= (k, 0, 0) \end{align} $$ With the reflection mirror at \(\mathbf{r}_1 = (0, l_1, 0),\mathbf{r}_2 = (l_2, 0, 0)\), before the recombination, the electric fields of the waves at the back of the beamsplitter are $$ \begin{align} E_1^{(BS)} &= \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} E_1^{(0)} e^{i(2k l_1 - \omega t)} = \frac{1}{2} E^{(0)} e^{i(2k l_1 - \omega t)} \ E_2^{(BS)} &= \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} E_2^{(0)} e^{i(2k l_2 - \omega t)} = \frac{1}{2} E^{(0)} e^{i(2k l_2 - \omega t)} \end{align} $$ where another factor of \(1/\sqrt{2}\) is introduced because they pass through the beamsplitter before they recombine (basically got splitted again). The combined field is then $$ \begin{align} E^{(BS)} &= E_1^{(BS)} + E_2^{(BS)} \ &= \frac{1}{2} E^{(0)} \left[e^{i(2k l_1 - \omega t)} + e^{i(2k l_2 - \omega t)}\right] \ &= \frac{1}{2} E^{(0)} e^{-i \omega t} \left(e^{2i k l_1} + e^{2i k l_2}\right) \end{align} $$ We can simplify the expression by defining $$ \bar{l} = \frac{l_1 + l_2}{2}, \quad \Delta l = l_1 - l_2 $$ so it becomes $$ \begin{align} E^{(BS)} &= \frac{1}{2} E^{(0)} e^{-i \omega t} \left[e^{2i k (\bar{l} + \Delta l/2)} + e^{2i k (\bar{l} - \Delta l/2)}\right] \ &= \frac{1}{2} E^{(0)} e^{-i \omega t} e^{2i k \bar{l}} \left(e^{i k \Delta l} + e^{-i k \Delta l}\right) \ &= E^{(0)} e^{-i \omega t} e^{2i k \bar{l}} \cos(k \Delta l) \end{align} $$

    Intensity

    The intensity of the un-simplified field is $$ \begin{align} I &\propto |E^{(BS)} E^{(BS)}| \ &= \frac{1}{4} |E_0^{(0)} E_0^{(0)}| \left(e^{2i k l_1} + e^{2i k l_2}\right) \left(e^{-2i k l_1} + e^{-2i k l_2}\right) \ &= \frac{I_0}{4} \left[2 + e^{2i k (l_1 - l_2)} + e^{-2i k (l_1 - l_2)}\right] \ &= \frac{I_0}{2} \left{1 + \cos\left[2k (l_1 - l_2)\right]\right} \ &= I_0 \cos^2\left[k (l_1 - l_2)\right] \ &= I_0cos^2(k\Delta l) \quad \text{(from the defining \(l\))} \end{align} $$
