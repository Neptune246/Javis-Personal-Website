    Phenomenology of metals and properties of Drude model

    Drude model

    A model that is used as a model for metal. In Drude model, electrons in a metal are treated as a free Fermi gas, occupying states up to the Fermi energy. The occupied states then form a sphere called Fermi sphere in k-space.

    Resistivity: $\(\rho = \frac{RA}{L}\)$ Similar to Ohm's law \(V=IR\), the current density is $\(J = \sigma E\)$ where \(\sigma\) is the conductivity and \(E = J\rho = J/\sigma\) is electric field.

    For Drude model, we can modify \(I = nAqv\) into \(J = nqv_d\) where \(v_d\) is the drift velocity and $\(F = qE = ma = \frac{mv_d}{\tau}\)$ where \(\tau\) is the collision time between the electrons and the atoms. From these we get $\(J = \frac{nq^2\tau E}{m} = \sigma E\)$ where \(\sigma = nq^2\tau /m\)

    Thermal conductivity

    Drude model predicts thermal conductivity using the kinetic theory of gases. We have \(\underline{J}_q = -\kappa \underline{\nabla}T\) where \(\kappa = (1/3) nC\lambda <v> = (1/3) nC<v>^2 \tau\) . Using \(C_v = \frac{3}{2} nk_B\) (from classical equipartition) and \(v_{d}^2 = \frac{3k_B T}{m}\), we get:

    \[ \kappa = \frac{n k_B^2 T \tau}{m} \]

    Wiedemann - Franz ratio: $\(\frac{\kappa}{\sigma T} = \text{const} \approx \frac{3}{2}\frac{k_B^2}{e^2} = L\quad(\text{Lorentz number)}\)$ This matches the experiments surprisingly well, even though the Drude model's assumptions are oversimplified. However, the reality is actually larger, showing the inadequate accuracy of Drude model (because two errors cancel out)

    Heat Capacity

    Phonons contribute \(AT^3\) and electron contribute \(\gamma T\). However, Drude model can't explain this phenomena so it fails. Drude model also gets the mean free path of electron and magnetic susceptibility wrong.

    Free electron model: Fermi gas model

    We consider this model.

    It's the combination of electrons in a box + Pauli principle where there is no interactions with others.

    By solving the Schrodinger equation, we know $\(E = \frac{\hbar^2k^2}{2m}\)$ and \(p = \hbar k\)

    We then use Pauli principle to visualise the energy of the electrons where \(E_F = \hbar^2k_F^2/2m\) is the Fermi energy.
