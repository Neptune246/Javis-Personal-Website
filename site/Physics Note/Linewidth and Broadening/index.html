    Linewidth and Broadening

    The aim of this is to minimise the spread of this graph (Probability of the atom to be in excited state vs the detuning frequency \(\Delta\) ).

    Interpretation of this graph

    Detuning frequency (\(\Delta\) = \(\omega - \omega_{12}\)) measures how well the oscillator (laser/microwave) frequency is to match the natural resonance frequency of the atom.

    [!Goal] To minimise the spread of this graph so that we know the real natural resonance frequency for laser calibration.

    This graph is basically telling us how well does the atom response to the oscillator when the frequency of oscillator is slightly off the natural resonance frequency. A broad line means the atom responses to a range of wavelengths, which makes us difficult to determine the real resonance frequency.

    Problems When Narrowing the Line

    1. Transit time broadening
    2. The atoms move pass the oscillator quickly and the time for interacting with the light field is limited

    [!Solution] - Use laser cooling to slow down the atoms when passing through the oscillator - Use magneto-optical trap to trap the atoms

    1. Doppler Broadening
    2. The atoms could be moving towards or away from the oscillator
    3. When moving towards, the atoms see higher frequency (\(\omega = \omega_{12} \left( 1 + \frac{v_z}{c} \right)\))

    4. When moving away, the atoms see lower frequency (\(\omega = \omega_{12} \left( 1 - \frac{v_z}{c} \right)\))

    5. Broadening areas due to Maxwell-Boltzmann distribution of velocities
      $$ P(v_z) \, dv_z = \left( \frac{M}{2\pi k_B T} \right)^{\frac{1}{2}} \exp\left(-\frac{Mv_z^2}{2k_BT}\right) \, dv_z $$

    → Distribution of frequencies
    $$ P(\omega) \, d\omega = \frac{c}{\omega_2} \left( \frac{M}{2\pi k_B T} \right)^{\frac{1}{2}} \exp\left(-\frac{Mc^2}{2k_BT} \frac{(\omega - \omega_2)^2}{\omega_2^2}\right) \, d\omega $$

    → FWHM
    $$ \Delta \omega_D = \frac{2\omega_2}{c} \left( \frac{2k_B T}{M} \ln 2 \right)^{\frac{1}{2}} $$

    \[ = 7.16 \times 10^{-7} \omega_2 \left( \frac{I}{M_A} \right)^{\frac{1}{2}} \]

    mass in a.m.u

    [!Solution] Use laser cooling, magneto-optical trap or optical tweezer

    1. Natural Broadening
    2. The atoms will spontaneously de-excite from the excited state

    [!Solution] - Use a longer half-life atom - Low freq magnetic dipole transition - hyperfine structure transition

    The decay rate of the excited state is given by Einstein A-coefficient Rate, \(A_{21}\)

    \[ A_{21} = \frac{d^2 \omega_{12}^3}{3\pi \epsilon_0 \hbar c^3} \left( \frac{g_1}{g_2} \right) $$ where $d^2 = | \langle 1|er|2\rangle|^2$ and $r$ is the radius of atom. The *natural lifetime* is then $$\tau = \frac{1}{\Gamma} = \frac{1}{A_{21}}\]
