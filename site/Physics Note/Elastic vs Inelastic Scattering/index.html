    Elastic vs Inelastic Scattering

    We want to investigate if a scattering event is elastic or not. If the collision is elastic then the momentum are fully conserved i.e. $\(w^\mu = p^\mu_e + p^\mu_P - p'^\mu_e = p_p^\mu + q^\mu\)$ where \(q^\mu = p^\mu_e - p_e'^\mu\).

    We want to find \(Q^2\) and plot a graph to see what is the difference between elastic and inelastic scattering. We first define the velocity of proton: $$ \nu = \frac{p_P^\mu \cdot q^\mu}{M_P} = \frac{(M_P, \mathbf{0}) \cdot (E - E', \mathbf{p} - \mathbf{p}')}{M_P} = \frac{E - E'}{M_P} $$

    and we find $\({(w^\mu)}^2 = {(p^\mu_e + p^\mu_P - p'^\mu_e)}^2 = {p_p^\mu}^2 + 2M_p\nu - Q^2\)$

    For elastic scattering, \((w^\mu)^2 = M_p^2 = (p_p^\mu)^2 \implies 2M_p\nu - Q^2 = 0\)

    For inelastic scattering, proton absorbs energy from electron to excite and accelerate: \((w^\mu)^2 > M_p^2 = (p_p^\mu)^2 \implies 2M_p\nu - Q^2 > 0\)

    Experimentally, the inelastic lineshape will be just a peak but since the energy of the electron will be used to excite the protons, so there will be many resonances. \(\Delta^+\) resonance is the first excited state of proton.

    It will be great to quantify if the scattering is elastic or not. Using the fact that \(2M_p\nu - Q^2 = 0\) for elastic and \(2M_p\nu - Q^2 > 0\) for inelastic scattering. We define:

    $\(\chi = \frac{Q^2}{2M_p\nu}\)$ where \(0 \leq X \leq 1\) . Therefore, the Rosenbluth cross-section can be modified to include \(X\) $$ \frac{d^2\sigma}{d(Q^2) d\chi} = \frac{4\pi\alpha^2}{Q^4} \left[ \left(1 - y - \frac{M_p^2 y^2 \chi^2}{Q^2}\right) \frac{F_2(\chi, Q^2)}{\chi} + y^2 F_1(\chi, Q^2) \right] $$ where \(y = (P_P \cdot q)/(P_p \cdot P_e)\) and \(F_1\) and \(F_2\) are analogous to \(g_E\) and \(g_M\).
