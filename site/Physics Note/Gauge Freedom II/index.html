    Gauge Freedom II

    Following from Gauge Freedom I that the solution to the Hamiltonian for a charged particle with mass in \(E\) and \(B\) field is separable. For solving for the Hamiltonian $$ \left[ -\frac{\hbar^2}{2m} \frac{\partial^2}{\partial y^2} + \underbrace{\frac{m}{2} \underbrace{\frac{q^2 B^2}{m^2}}{\omega_c^2} \left( y - \underbrace{\frac{\hbar k}{qB}} \right] \phi(y) = E \phi(y) $$ where the second term is potential energy of a SHO (} \right)^2}_{\text{potential for SHO}\(U = m\omega^2 x^2 /2\) centred at \(y_0\) with the cyclotron frequency \(\omega_c = qB/m\) . The energy of this system is quantised so when we solve for energy, we have $\(E_n = \left(n+\frac{1}{2}\right)\hbar \omega_c = \left(n+\frac{1}{2}\right) \frac{\hbar qB}{m}\)$

    Note that gauge freedom will change the Hamiltonian but will not change the observable, i.e. the observables are gauge invariant.

    \(y_0\) periodicity

    Now we investigate the value of \(y_0\). We first trap the motion of the charged particle in a 2D square plane with \(L\) on each side and a \(\mathbf{B}\)-field out of the page.

    Since \(y_0 = \hbar k / qB\) is dependent on \(k\), alongside with the fact that the energy is quantised, it means that these wave vectors shows the number of independent quantum states.

    Since the motion is constraints in \(L\) and can be repeated on an infinite 2D plane, we expect a periodicity in \(y_0\) $$ e^{-ik(x+L)} = e^{-ikx} \implies e^{-ikL} = 1 \implies kL = 2\pi l \quad \text{where } l \in \mathbb{Z}_+ $$

    and we find $$ y_0 = \frac{\hbar k}{qB} = \frac{\hbar \cdot 2\pi l}{qBL} \quad \forall l \in \mathbb{Z_+} $$ We realise \(y_0\) is constrained between \(0\) to \(L\) so: $$ 0 < \frac{\hbar \cdot 2\pi l}{qBL} < L $$

    By rearranging for \(l\), we have $$ 0 < l < l_{\text{max}} = \frac{\Phi}{\frac{h}{q}} $$ and this is the range of allowed values for \(y_0\) where \(\Phi = B\cdot L^2\) is the magnetic flux.

    Aharanov-Bohm Effect

    In quantum mechanics, it is the scalar and vector potentials that describe the behaviour of particles as compared to the \(E\) and \(B\) in classical mechanics.

    To see this we compare two situations:

    \(B\)-field without constraint

    Consider a particle moving circularly with a \(B\)-field in the \(z\) axis. We use cylindrical coordinates (\(\rho, \phi, z\)) and express Hamiltonian as $$ H = -\frac{\hbar^2 \nabla^2}{2m} $$ where $$ \nabla^2 = \frac{1}{\rho} \frac{\partial}{\partial \rho} \left( \rho \frac{\partial}{\partial \rho} \right) + \frac{1}{\rho^2} \frac{\partial^2}{\partial \phi^2} + \frac{\partial^2}{\partial z^2} $$ By solving it we get $$ \psi(\phi) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}} e^{in\phi}, \quad E_n = \frac{\hbar^2 n^2}{2mb^2}, \quad n \in \mathbb{Z} $$

    Constrained \(B\)-field

    Now we constrain the \(B\)-field in a solenoid of radius \(a\) at the centre of the particle orbit. The \(B\)-field is non-zero only in the solenoid. Then the Hamiltonian is modified to $$ H = \frac{1}{2m} \left( -i\hbar \nabla - q\vec{A} \right)^2 $$ where we need \(\vec{A}\) to be zero outside because \(\vec{B} = \nabla \times \vec{A}\), therefore we need $$ \vec{A} = A_\phi(\rho)\hat{\phi} = \begin{cases} \dfrac{\Phi\rho}{2\pi a^2}\hat{\phi} = \dfrac{B\rho}{2}\hat{\phi} & \text{if } \rho < a, \[10pt] \dfrac{\Phi}{2\pi\rho}\hat{\phi} = \dfrac{Ba^2}{2\rho}\hat{\phi} & \text{if } \rho \geq a. \end{cases} $$ where $$ \vec{B} = \nabla \times \vec{A} = \begin{cases} B\hat{z} & \text{if } \rho < a, \ 0 & \text{if } \rho \geq a. \end{cases} $$

    By solving for this Hamiltonian, we get $$ E_n = \frac{\hbar^2}{2mb^2} \left[ n - \frac{\Phi}{(h/q)} \right]^2 $$ Note that how the energy level depends on magnetic flux even though there is no magnetic field outside the solenoid.
