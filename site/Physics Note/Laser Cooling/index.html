    Laser Cooling

    Introduction

    Maxwell-Boltzmann curve gives the probability-velocity curve. Laser cooling the velocity of the distribution to 'cool' the temperature. Note that we always want the atoms to decay from the excited to ground state i.e. closed loop transition without needing a repumping laser.

    Consider atom travelling in \(+z\) direction towards a laser beam with \(\omega_L\).

    The frequency experienced by atom is $$ \omega = \omega_L \left( 1 + \frac{v_z}{c} \right) $$

    To slow the atom, we red detune the laser so that the atom will reach resonance with the laser frequency, described by formula: $$ \Delta = \omega_{12} - \omega_L = \frac{\omega_L v_z}{c} = k v_z $$

    [!Definition] Red detuning: Setting the laser frequency to slightly below the resonance frequency

    However, the atom will slow down when in resonance with the laser, causing it to shift away from the red-detuning laser.

    Compensation

    1. Change the laser frequency over time (Chirp cooling) but this can only work with pulses of atoms, not continuous beams.
    2. Shift the resonance frequency with position to keep the atoms in resonance as they slow down (Zeeman Deceleration)

    [!Zeeman Deceleration] - To compensate Doppler shift, a position-dependent magnetic field is applied to Zeeman-shift the atomic resonance. - The magnetic field is set such that $\(\omega_{12} + \underbrace{\frac{\mu_B B(z)}{\hbar}}_{\text{frequency of the magnetic field } (\hbar\omega = \mu_B B(z))} = \omega_L + kv_z\)$ - Another equation can be derived $\(B(z) = B_0 \left( 1 - \frac{z}{L_0} \right)^{\frac{1}{2}} + B_{\text{bias}}\)$ - Without \(B_{bias}\), the magnetic field will go to zero at the end so we need to ensure a nonzero magnetic field everywhere. So we set \(\mu_B B_{bias} \approx \hbar\omega_L - \hbar\omega_{12}\), then the atoms come to rest at \(z=L_0\) - Achieved experimentally with a tapered solenoid - Useful for cooling fast atoms such as He/Li and atoms with low vapour pressure

    Recall the scattering force in Light Forces $$ F = \frac{\Gamma}{2} \frac{I}{I + I_{\text{sat}} \left( 1 + \frac{4\Delta^2}{\Gamma^2} \right)} $$

    If the atom has velocity \(v_z\) we need to include the doppler shift in the detuning term $$ F_\pm = \pm \frac{\hbar k \Gamma}{2} \frac{I}{I + I_{\text{sat}} \left( 1 + \frac{4(\Delta I k v_z)^2}{\Gamma^2} \right)} $$

    Then the total force (\(F = F_+ +F_-\)) on atom is shown in with capture range \(\approx \pm \Delta/k\)

    Doppler cooling limit

    • Lowest temperature can possibly achieve

    Diffusion: $$ T_{\text{min}} = \frac{\hbar \Gamma}{2k_B} \frac{1 + \left( \frac{2\Delta}{\Gamma} \right)^2}{-2\Delta / \Gamma} $$ Minimum is at \(\Delta = -\Gamma/2\) so we have $\(T_{min}= \frac{\hbar\Gamma}{2k_B}\)$

    Doppler velocity

    • Velocity corresponding to a Doppler shift equal to natural linewidth \(\Gamma\)
    • Beyond this velocity, the atom is significantly off resonance
    • Equate Kinetic energy to \(\hbar\Gamma/2\)

    Recoil velocity

    • Velocity an atom acquires from absorbing or emitting a single photon

    Calculated from using the conservation of momentum $\(mv_r = \hbar k\)$

    Capture velocity

    • Maximum initial velocity at which an atom can still be slowed and trapped by a given laser-cooling configuration
    \[v_c \approx \frac{\Gamma}{k}\]
