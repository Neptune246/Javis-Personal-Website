    Finding the Hydrogen Wavefunction

    The radial equation for a spherical potential

    Based on the spherical polar, the Schrodinger equation now becomes $$ \frac{1}{R(r)}\frac{d}{dr}\left(r^{2}\frac{dR(r)}{dr}\right) - \frac{2mr^{2}}{\hbar^{2}}\left(V(r) - E\right) = \frac{L^{2}Y(\theta,\phi)}{\hbar^{2}Y(\theta,\phi)} $$ By using the eigenvalue equation of total angular momentum and substituting \(U_{nl} = rR_{nl}\) we find that the radial equation $$ -\frac{\hbar^2}{2m} \frac{d^2 U_{nl}}{dr^2} + \left[ V(r) + \frac{\hbar^2 l(l+1)}{2mr^2} \right] U_{nl} = E U_{nl} $$ is analogous to that of 1D Schrodinger equation with the potential replaced by \(V(r) + \hbar^2 l(l+1)/2mr^2 = V(r) + {L^2}/2mr^2\). \(n\) in this case is the principle quantum number. The additional term behaves like a contribution to the potential can be appreciated by considering an electron on a circular orbit.

    The hydrogen atom

    For a hydrogen atom with a proton and an electron, the coulomb potential \(V(r) = -Ze^2/(4\pi \epsilon_0 r)\), by substituting this into the radial equation above: $$ -\frac{\hbar^2}{2\mu}\frac{d^2U_{nl}}{dr^2} + \left[\frac{l(l+1)\hbar^2}{2\mu r^2} - \frac{Ze^2}{4\pi\epsilon_0 r}\right]U_{nl} = EU_{nl} $$ where \(\mu = M_p m_e / (M_p + m_e)\) is reduced mass. Solving the Laguerre equation with \(\rho = kr\) and \(k = \mu Z e^2 / (4\pi \epsilon_0 \hbar^2 n)\) for \(n = 1, 2, 3 ...\) yields $$ E \approx -13.6 \, \frac{Z^2}{n^2} \left( \frac{\mu}{\mu_H} \right) \, \text{eV} $$ For hydrogen, \(\mu_H = \mu\) and \(Z = 1\)

    Through the derivation of this energy equation, \(l\) can be linked to \(n\) by $\(l = 0, 1, ..., n-1\)$

    Degeneracy

    • For a given \(n\), there are \(n\) values of \(l\) which have the same energy, hence the level is \(n\) degenerate
    • For each \(l\), there are also \(2l +1\) degenerate values of \(m\)

    The Laguerre equation gives solutions of \(R_{10}(r) = 2 a^{-3/2} e^{-r/a}\) \(R_{20}(r) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} a^{-3/2} \left(1 - \frac{r}{2a} \right) e^{-r/2a}\) \(R_{21}(r) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{24}} a^{-3/2} \frac{r}{a} e^{-r/2a}\)

    Radial probability density

    By using the full wavefunction of \(\psi_{nlm} = R_{nl}Y_{lm}\), the radial probability density is then $$ P(r)\,dr = \int_{\phi=0}^{2\pi} \int_{\theta=0}^{\pi} R_{nl}^2 Y_{lm}^* Y_{lm} r^2 \sin\theta \, d\theta \, d\phi \, dr = R_{nl}^2 r^2 \, dr $$
