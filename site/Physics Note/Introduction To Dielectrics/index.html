    Introduction To Dielectrics

    [!Definition of Dielectrics] Dielectrics are insulator materials that can be polarised by an applied electric field. $\(C= \frac{Q}{V} = \epsilon_0\epsilon_r\frac{A}{d}\)$ where \(\epsilon_r\) is the dielectric constant

    This is the electric analogue of diamagnets with equations here. When an atom is being polarised by an electric field, it forms an electric dipole moment by deforming its electron cloud, governed by formula $\(|\mu| = qd\)$ and torque $\(\mathbf{\tau} = \mu \times \mathbf{E}\)$ and potential energy due to electric field $\(U = -\mu \cdot \mathbf{E}\)$

    Dielectric Media In An Electric Field

    The torque induces rotations in the dipole such that the positive side aligns with the electric field

    and the polarisation (magnetisation in magnetism) is defined as the dipole moment per unit volume $\(\mathbf{P} = N\mu\)$ The electric field due to a single dipole is $\(\mathbf{E}(\mathbf{r}) = \frac{3(\boldsymbol{\mu} \cdot \mathbf{r})\mathbf{r} - r^2 \boldsymbol{\mu}}{4\pi \epsilon_0 r^5}\)$ and the uniform polarisation \(\mathbf{P}\), the collective effect of all dipoles is the surface charge density $\(\sigma = \mathbf{P} \cdot \mathbf{\hat{n}}\)$ The polarisability, \(\alpha\) (sensitivity of a dipole to external electric field) of a single dipole is $\(\mu = \alpha\mathbf{E}_{\text{local}}\)$

    Local Electric Field Of a Dipole

    To calculate this, we need to cut a spherical segment of dipole and calculate this separately.

    Given that the Lorentz field is $\(\mathbf{E}_2 = \frac{\mathbf{P}}{3\epsilon_0}\)$ and atomistic field is, as suggested above, $\(\begin{aligned} \mathbf{E}_3 &= \sum_{\text{dipoles } i} \frac{3(\boldsymbol{\mu}_i \cdot \mathbf{r}_i)\mathbf{r}_i - r_i^2 \boldsymbol{\mu}_i}{4\pi \epsilon_0 r_i^5} \\ &= \mu \sum_{i} \frac{3x_i^2 - r_i^2}{4\pi \epsilon_0 r_i^5} = 0 \end{aligned}\)$ where we have assume \(\mu_i = \mu\) is parallel to the \(x\) axis and $\(\sum_{i} x_i^2 = \sum_{i} y_i^2 = \sum_{i} z_i^2 = \frac{1}{3} \sum_{i} r_i^2\)$ Therefore we have $\(\mathbf{E}_{\text{local}} = \mathbf{E}_{\text{ext}} - \frac{\sigma}{\epsilon_0} + \frac{P}{3\epsilon_0}\)$
