    Exchange Operators and Pauli Exclusion Principle

    Here we introduce the exchange operator and how it relates to Pauli Exclusion Principle.

    Consider a system of two identical, indistinguishable particles described by $\(\psi(\underbrace{\vec{r}_1, \sigma_1}_{1}; \underbrace{\vec{r}_2, \sigma_2}_{2}) \equiv \psi(1, 2)\)$ If we introduce an exchange operator on the wave function, we, mathematically, exchange the label of the particles, i.e. the positions, momenta and spins of the particles. $\(P_{12}\psi(1,2)=\psi(2,1)\)$

    Properties of \(P\) on Hamiltonian

    Since the particles are identical, the Hamiltonian has to be invariant under the exchange of particle labels \(\psi(1,2)=\psi(2,1)\). Therefore if we apply the exchange operator on both sides and multiply both sides by \(P_{12}\), we get

    \[P_{12}H(1,2) \cancelto{\mathbb{I}}{P_{12}^{-1}P_{12}} = H(2,1)P_{12}$$ $$\implies [P_{12}, H(1,2)] = 0\]

    The commutativity implies that we can find a common eigenvector for \(P\) and \(H\): $\(P_{12}\psi=\lambda\psi\)$

    To find the eigenvalue of \(P_{12}\), we use the fact that \(P^2 = \mathbb{I}\) (I have proven it) as exchanging twice gives doesn't do shit on the original wavefunction. $\(P \cdot P \psi = P \lambda \psi \implies \mathbb{I}\psi = \lambda(\lambda\psi)\)$ $\(\implies \lambda^2 = 1\)$

    Bosons and Fermions

    If \(\lambda =1\) (exchanging labels don't affect their wave function) then we have \(P_{12} \psi(1,2) = \psi(2,1) = \psi(1,2)\). If we construct a wavefunction consisting two states in the system, we have $\(\psi(r, r') = \frac{\phi_1(r)\phi_2(r')+\phi_1(r')\phi_2(r)}{\sqrt{2}}\)$ where \(\phi_i\) is the wavefunction of individual particle. They are allowed to be in the same state i.e. BOSONS

    If we have \(\lambda = -1\) (exchanging labels makes the wavefunction antisymmetric) then we have $\(\psi(r, r') = \frac{\phi_1(r)\phi_2(r')-\phi_1(r')\phi_2(r)}{\sqrt{2}}\)$ which gives zero wave function i.e. FERMIONS (Pauli Exclusion principle)

    In summary: exchanging the labels of two indistinguishable particles can have two impacts on the wavefunction. 1. Nothing 2. Make the total wavefunction anti-symmetric
