    Absorption cross section and saturation

    Motivation:

    Absorption cross-section refers to how likely an atom to absorb a photon by looking at the intensity change of a beam as it passes through a sample of atoms

    Saturation here refers to how populated the excited state is.

    Here we look at how intense light affects the energy levels.

    We first incorporate natural broadening in rate equations as defined in Light Forces because spontaneous emission leads natural broadening.

    \[ a_{21}(\omega) = A_{21} L(\omega) \]
    \[ b_{21}(\omega) = B_{21} L(\omega) \]
    \[ b_{12}(\omega) = B_{12} L(\omega) \]

    where \(L(\omega)\) is normalised Lorentzian lineshape. This means that atom-light interaction is spread over a range of frequencies with a maximum on resonance \(L(\omega_{12}) = 2/\pi\Gamma\).

    Absorption cross-section

    We want to know how the spectral intensity of the beam changes as it passes through a thin sample of atoms. Here we assume steady state (no spontaneous emission).

    [!Definition] Spectral intensity: Optical power per unit area per unit angular frequency

    \[ \left[ I(z + dz) - I(z) \right] A \, d\omega = \left[ N_2 B_{21} L(\omega) \rho(\omega) \, d\omega - N_1 B_{12} L(\omega) \rho(\omega) \, d\omega \right] \, \hbar \omega A \, dz \]

    [!Interpretation] LHS: Optical power and it is related to the energy of the electromagnetic waves. RHS: Rate of the change in EM energy (power) due to the photons (energy of net rate of transition per unit volume)

    In the limit \(dz \to 0\),
    $$ \frac{1}{I} \frac{dI}{dz} = \frac{\hbar \omega}{c} \left[ N_2 B_{21} - N_1 B_{12} \right] L(\omega) $$

    Substitute for \(B\) coefficient using \(A_{21} = \frac{\hbar \omega_{12}^3}{\pi^2 c^3} B_{21}\) and \(g_1 B_{12} = g_2 B_{21}\),
    $$ \frac{1}{I} \frac{dI}{dz} = \underbrace{\frac{\pi^2 c^2}{\omega_{12}^2} A_{21} L(\omega)}_{\text{Scattering (absorption) cross section } (\sigma(\omega))} \left[ N_2 - N_1 \right] $$ (Textbook interpretation see here)

    [!note] This is the result for unpolarised light. For polarised light, we have three polarisation axes so we have to multiply by three.

    And the solution to this equation is the beer's law $\(I(z) = I(0)e^{\sigma(\omega)[N_2-N_1]z} = I(0)e^{-\kappa(\omega)z}\)$ Remember that we are dealing with intense light when stimulated emission is significant (which affects \(N_2\)). Unlike weak beam where \(N_1\) and \(N_2\) are roughly the same. Physical intuition: stimulated emission leads to gain in intensity.

    Saturation

    \[ \underbrace{(N_1 - N_2) \sigma(\omega) I}_{\text{Net absorption rate}} = \underbrace{N_2 A_{21} \hbar\omega}_{\text{Rate of atoms scatter energy out of the beam}} \]

    Where: - \(N_1, N_2\) = populations of ground and excited states - \(\sigma(\omega)\) = absorption cross section - \(I\) = beam intensity - \(A_{21}\) = Einstein A coefficient (spontaneous emission rate)

    The power absorbed from the beam = power radiated away by atoms

    \[ r = \frac{\sigma(\omega)I}{A_{21} \hbar\omega}, \quad (N_1 - N_2)r = N_2, \quad N_1 + N_2 = N \]
    \[ (N_1 - N_2) = \frac{N}{1 + 2r} = \frac{N}{1 + \frac{I}{I_S(\omega)}} \]

    When \(I = I_S\), \(\frac{1}{4}\) of atoms are excited.

    Solution method

    \[ I_S(\omega) = \frac{\hbar\omega A_{21}}{2\sigma(\omega)} \]

    → Minimum value of \(I_S\) occurs on resonance when \(\sigma(\omega)\) is largest (when the interaction is very probable).

    \[ I_S(\omega_0) = I_{\text{sat}} = \frac{\pi\hbar c}{3\lambda^2 \tau} \]

    Power Broadening

    By subsituting \((N_1 - N_2)\) above into the beer's law, we get

    \[\kappa(\omega,I) = \frac{N\sigma(\omega)}{1+\frac{I}{I_S(\omega)}}\]

    and for whatever the fuck reason, it becomes $$ k(\omega, I) = \frac{N\sigma(\omega_{12})\frac{\Gamma^2}{4}}{(\omega - \omega_{12})^2 + \frac{\Gamma^2}{4}} \times \frac{1}{1 + \frac{I}{I_{\text{sat}}}\left(\frac{\frac{\Gamma^2}{4}}{(\omega - \omega_{12})^2 + \frac{\Gamma^2}{4}}\right)} $$

    That means the absorption coefficient has a Lorentzian lineshape, implying Power Broadening. This is due to saturation which reduces absorption near resonance while far from resonance the absorption changes little.

    Therefore the scattering force is generalised to $$ F = \hbar k R_{\text{scatt}} = \hbar k \frac{dN_2}{dt} $$

    \[ = \hbar k \frac{\Gamma}{2} \frac{I}{I + I_{\text{sat}}\left(1 + \frac{4\Delta^2}{\Gamma^2}\right)} \]

    At low intensity: \(F\propto I\) At high intensity: \(F_{max} = \hbar k\Gamma / 2\) as discussed in Light Forces
