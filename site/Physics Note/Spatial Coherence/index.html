    Spatial Coherence

    • Traverse coherence length is the lateral distance over which knowledge of phase can be predicted

    Van Cittert - Zernike Theorem

    In this case, we cannot predict the wave front using time because it is corrugated. So we use Young's double slit to test spatial coherence.

    For a narrow slit, coherence is greater than the separation between the two points in the second plane which gives interference.

    What we observe is that as we increase the slit width, the visibility drops to zero and reappear again

    and we also observe that the visibility graph is the fourier transform of the aperture function i.e. rect in this case. By ignoring diffraction, the on axis light is $\(\mathcal{E} = \mathcal{E}_1 e^{\frac{ikdx}{2z}} + \mathcal{E}_2 e^{-\frac{ikdx}{2z}}\)$ where \(d\sin \theta \approx dx/2z\) and the factor of two in the denominator is because we can take the angle from the top side of the single slit and bottom side of the slit.

    If the slit is displaced by a distance \(x_s\), the terms above gain an additional phase and the we get $\(I = 4\bar{I}_s \cos^2 \left[ \frac{kd}{2} \left( \frac{x}{z} + \frac{x_s}{z_s} \right) \right]\)$ Each point in aperture gives cos squared fringes. To see the effect of the width of the single slit, we need to integrate the equation over all point and we get $\(I = 2\tilde{I} \left[ 1 + |\gamma_{12}| \cos \left( \frac{kdx}{z} \right) \right]\)$where $\(V = |\gamma_{12}| = \frac{\Gamma_{12}(k)}{\underbrace{\Gamma_{12}(0)}_{\text{max value}}} \quad , \quad \Gamma_{12}(k) = \underbrace{\int_{0}^{\infty} f(x_s) e^{-\frac{ikd}{z_s} x_s} dx_s}_\text{FT of the source}\)$ where \(f(x_s)\) is the aperture function. Therefore: Visibility of fringes = modulus of fourier transform of the source

    ** Another approach to see this is that, for a fixed slit width, the separation of the two pinole gives the visibility

    The idea is that if the light from LHS slit diffracts and covers the area of the RHS slit, in QM, we don't know which one passes through, therefore there is no interference.

    If the area of the light patch does not cover both slits, then in QM, we know which one goes through, therefore, no interference.

    The size of the light patch on screen is \(\theta z = \lambda z/a_s\) so if we want to see fringes, we need $\(\frac{\lambda}{a_s}z > d\)$ The coherence length is then $\(\frac{\lambda z}{a_s}\)$ and the area is $\(A_c = \frac{(\lambda z)^2}{A_s} = \frac{\lambda^2}{\Omega_s}\)$ where \(\Omega_s\) is the solid angle subtended.
