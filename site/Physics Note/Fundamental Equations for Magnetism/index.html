    Fundamental Equations for Magnetism

    Consider an electron moving in a circular loop, current is generated and it creates the magnetic field through the normal of the plane of the plane. The magnetic field acts as a magnetic dipole and it has a magnetic moment of $\(\mathbf{{\mu}} = I\mathbf{A}\)$ and the angular momentum of the electron is then (solved classically): $$ \mathbf{L} = \mathbf{r} \times \mathbf{p} = m(\mathbf{r} \times \mathbf{v}) $$ where \(\mathbf{p}\) is the momentum. The magnetic moment can also be written as $\(\mathbf{\mu} = -\gamma \mathbf{L}\)$ where \(\gamma = e/2m\) is the gyromagnetic ratio.

    The energy of the magnetic moment is then $\(E = -\mathbf{\mu} \cdot \mathbf{B}\)$ - Since in quantum mechanics, the angular momentum is \(\mathbf{L}=m_l\hbar\) we have $$ E_L = \gamma L_z B = \underbrace{\gamma \hbar}_{\mu_B} m_L B $$ - For spin, \(\mu_s = -g_s\gamma \mathbf{s}\), we have spin energy $\(E_s=-\mu_s\cdot\mathbf{B} = g_sm_s\mu_BB = \pm\mu_B B\)$ for electron with \(g_s \approx 2\) and \(m_s = \pm 1/2\)

    Aside:

    For spin-orbit interaction, the energy is \(\Delta E \propto (dV/dr) (\mathbf{s} \cdot \mathbf{L})\). As the atomic number increases, \(dV/dr\) increases and hence the strength of S-O coupling. For the total angular momentum, the magnetic moment is then $\(\mu_J = -g_J\gamma J\)$ where $\(g_J = \frac{3}{2}+\frac{s(s+1)-l(l+1)}{2j(j+1)}\)$

    Bohr Magneton

    • Fundamental unit for expressing magnetic moments
    • Magnetic moment for an electron in a circular loop $\(\mu_B = IA = \frac{ev}{2\pi r} \cdot \pi r^2 = \frac{e}{2m} (mvr) = \frac{e}{2m} L = \gamma L\)$ where \(I = e/T\) is current (rate of flow of charge) and \(L = mvr\) is the angular momentum.

    Magnetic fields

    In magnetism, we use \(\mathbf{B}\) and \(\mathbf{H}\) regularly. \(\mathbf{B}\) is the magnetic field and acts as a 'output' field while \(\mathbf{H}\) is the magnetic field from the outside to magnetise something.

    In vacuum, $\(\mathbf{B}=\mu_0 \mathbf{H}\)$ where \(\mu_0\) is the permeability of free space.

    In solid, $$ \mathbf{B} = \mu_0 (\mathbf{H} + \mathbf{M}) $$ where \(\mathbf{M}\) is the magnetisation (net magnetic moment per unit volume and is how strongly the material is magnetised by \(\mathbf{H}\)). In general, the relationship between \(\mathbf{B}\) and \(\mathbf{H}\) fields is $$ \mathbf{M} = \chi \mathbf{H} $$ where \(\chi\) is the susceptibility.

    • If diamagnet, \(\chi < 0\)
    • If paramagnet, \(\chi > 0\)
    • If ferromagnet, \(\chi \gg 0\)

    Contradiction

    If we consider the energy per unit volume of the magnetic moment, we have $$ M = -\frac{dE_{\text{vol}}}{dB} $$ but based on the Lorentz force \(\mathbf{F} = -e(\mathbf{v} \times \mathbf{B})\), the force is normal to the velocity of the electron, it does not do work on the electron, so the magnetisation should be zero.
