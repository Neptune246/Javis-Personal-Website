    Dielectric Function

    We first consider the Claussius-Mossotti relationship between relative permittivity to the polarisability. Let's start with polarisation (analogous to magnetisation)$\(P = N\mu = N\alpha E_{\text{local}}\)$ where \(\mu = \alpha E_{\text{local}}\) and \(E_{\text{local}} = E_{\text{macro}} + \frac{P}{3\epsilon_0}\) as derived here. From definition of electric displacement \(D = \epsilon_0 E_{\text{macro}} + P = \epsilon_0 \epsilon_r E_{\text{macro}}\): $\(\frac{\epsilon_r - 1}{\epsilon_r + 2} = \frac{N\alpha}{3\epsilon_0} \quad \text{(Claussius-Mossotti)}\)$ Here the \(E_{\text{macro}}\) is the internal macroscopic field i.e. sum of external and depolarising fields

    Electronic Polarisation and Clausius-Mossotti Relation

    Let's us take a look at one of the mechanism.

    When there is an oscillating local electric field: $\(\mathbf{E}_{\text{local}}(\omega) = \mathbf{E}_0 \exp(i\omega t)\)$ the electron position will be shifted in sync with the electric field: $\(\mathbf{r}(\omega) = \mathbf{r}_0 \exp(i\omega t)\)$ but since the nucleus of the atom is trying to pull the electron back to equilibrium $\(K = m\omega_0^2\)$ Now we write the force equation involving only two forces:

    [!note] $\(m \mathbf{\ddot{r}} = -K\mathbf{r} - e\mathbf{E}_{\text{local}}\)$

    By substituting the electric field and the electron position into the differential equation and solve it and consider the polarisability we get

    [!note] $\(\alpha(\omega) = \frac{e^2}{m(\omega_0^2 - \omega^2)}\)$ where \(\omega_0 = \sqrt{K/m}\)

    By using the Clausius-Mossotti we have $\(\epsilon_r(\omega) = 1 + \frac{Ne^2}{m\epsilon_0(\omega_0^2 - \omega^2) - (Ne^2/3)}\)$
