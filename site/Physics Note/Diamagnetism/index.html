    Diamagnetism

    Diamagnetism has a negative susceptibility as mentioned in Fundamental Equations for Magnetism, it implies that the \(B\)-field of the solid is smaller than the \(B\)-field of the vacuum, causing it to repel from a magnetic substance.

    As stated in Hund's Rule, diamagnetic substances have full shell (no unpaired electron), so it does not have magnetic moment without external field. Therefore we are calculating the susceptibility based on the fact (in quantum mechanics) that the external field will distort the orbital motion of electrons.

    Classical Physics

    We revisit the Larmor precession in Fundamental Equations for Magnetism and the basic equation of magnetic moment $\(\mu = I\mathbf{A} = -\frac{eB}{4\pi m} \pi \langle\rho^2\rangle\)$ where \(\langle \rho^2\rangle\) is the mean square radius and \(I = e/T = e/ (2\pi/\omega)\). Here we assume a spherically symmetric atom \(\langle r^2 \rangle = \langle x^2 \rangle + \langle y^2 \rangle + \langle z^2 \rangle\) and since \(\mu_z\) is unchanged, we have only two direction components \(\langle \rho^2 \rangle = \frac{2}{3} \langle r^2 \rangle\).

    Subs into the magnetic moment equation and assuming there are \(n\) atoms/ions per unit volume, each with \(Z\) electrons, we have $\(M = -\frac{n e^2 Z B \langle r^2 \rangle}{6m}\)$ For weak magnetisation, we have \(B = \mu_0 (H + M) \approx \mu_0 H \implies \chi = M/H = M/(B/\mu_0)\), giving $$ \chi = -\frac{n e^2 \mu_0 Z \langle r^2 \rangle}{6m} $$ Fucking ppiece of shittzshu nugget

    Quantum Mechanics

    We start from the electron Hamiltonian in a magnetic field $$ \hat{H} = \frac{(\mathbf{p} + e\mathbf{A})^2}{2m} + V + g_s \gamma \mathbf{B} \cdot \mathbf{s} $$which will essentially give $$ \hat{H} = \left( \frac{\mathbf{p}^2}{2m} + V \right) + \underbrace{\gamma \mathbf{B} \cdot (\mathbf{L} + g_s \mathbf{s})}{\text{paramagnetic term}} + \underbrace{\frac{e^2}{8m} (\mathbf{B} \times \mathbf{r})^2} $$}

    After identifying the diamagnetic term inside, we use first order perturbation theory to find the energy change to the ground state wavefunction $$ \Delta E = \frac{e^2}{8m} \langle 0 | (\mathbf{B} \times \mathbf{r})^2 | 0 \rangle = \frac{B^2 \langle 0 |(x^2 + y^2) | 0 \rangle}{8m} $$ Since \(\langle x^2 \rangle = \langle y^2 \rangle = \frac{\langle r^2 \rangle}{3}\), we have $$ \Delta E = \frac{e^2 B^2}{12m} \langle r^2 \rangle $$ For \(\mu = -\frac{d(\Delta E)}{dB}\), we have $$ M = -\frac{n e^2 Z B}{6m} \langle r^2 \rangle $$ $$ \chi = \frac{\mu_0 M}{B} = -\frac{n e^2 \mu_0 Z \langle r^2 \rangle}{6m} $$

    Magnetic Anisotropy

    For the diamagnetic term \((\mathbf{B}\times\mathbf{r})^2\) , the susceptibility is

    • Large when the magnetic field is perpendicular to the molecule
    • Small when the magnetic field is parallel to the molecule
