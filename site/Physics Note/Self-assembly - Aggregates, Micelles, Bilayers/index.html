    Self assembly Aggregates, Micelles, Bilayers

    Self assembly is a process when the molecules arrange themselves to form a complex structure like aggregation and formation of micelles and bilayers. Such arrangement is mainly driven by the free energy of the system, like nucleation and mixing and demixing

    Aggregates

    Consider a solute dissolving into a liquid, we want to predict it the aggregates form in the liquid. We have a volume fraction (fraction of the volume of solutes and the total contents in the liquid) of the solutes, \(\phi\).

    Say if aggregates form, each of them have exactly \(N\) solutes and occupies \(X_N\) volume fraction. We then have a relation: \(\sum_N X_N = \phi\) . For some reason and derivation is not known yet, the Gibb's free energy per molecule is $$ G_N = \mu_N = \epsilon_N + \frac{k_B T}{N} \ln \left( \frac{X_N}{N} \right) $$ where \(\varepsilon_N\) is the energy change per molecule when moving from bulk to an aggregate of size \(N\). Rearranging this gives $$ X_N = N \exp\left(\frac{N(\mu - \varepsilon_N)}{k_B T}\right) $$ By equating this to the case where \(N = 1\) and the equilibrium case where \(\mu = \mu_N = \mu_1 = \mu_2 = ...\)

    [!General Expression of Aggregates' Fraction Volume] $\(\implies X_N = N \left( X_1 e^{\frac{\epsilon_1 - \epsilon_N}{k_B T}} \right)^N\)$

    where \(\varepsilon_1 = \mu_N\) and \(X_1\) is the fraction of free solution molecules.

    • If \(\varepsilon_N > \varepsilon_1\), exponential term becomes negative and \(X_N\) decreases with energy, implying aggregates do not (primarily) form
    • If \(\varepsilon_N < \varepsilon_1\), aggregates form

    Another approach?

    Using the symbols with usual meanings in this note $$Nv = \frac{4}{3} \pi r^3 \implies r = \sqrt[3]{\frac{3v}{4\pi} N} $$ and $$ \epsilon_N = \frac{4 \pi r^2 \gamma}{N} + \epsilon_{\infty} $$ where \(v\) is the volume of solute and \(\varepsilon_\infty\) is introduced to avoid \(\varepsilon_N \rightarrow 0\) when \(N\) is large. Then by subs into \(X_N\) we get

    $$ X_N = N \left( X_1 e^{\alpha \left( 1 - N^{-1/3} \right)} \right)^N \approx N (X_1 e^{\alpha})^N $$ If \(X_1 < e^{-\alpha}\) there is almost no aggregates present. As the volume fraction increases and approaching \(e^{-\alpha}\) , aggregation must form as there is finite number of \(X_N\). This is called critical aggregation concentration where the aggregation starts to form.

    Aggregation of amphiphilic molecules

    • Amphiphilic molecules are molecules with hydrophilic and hydrophobic parts and they typically make up of a spherical headgroup with carbon chain(s).

    To form a spherical micelle, we have to fulfil several constraints. 1. Volume: \(4\pi r^3/3 = Mv\) 2. Surface area: \(4\pi r^2 = Ma_0\) 3. \(r \leq l_c\) $\(\frac{v_h}{l_c a_0} \leq \frac{1}{3}\)$ where \(a_0\) is the headgroup area, \(l_c\) is the critical chain length, \(v_h\) is the hydrocarbon volume and \(M\) is the number of molecules

    Critical micelle concentration

    To form a spherical micelle, the number of molecules cannot be too small or too large and there is a simple way to model this: assume a minimum in \(\varepsilon_N\) i.e. when the \(N=M\), \(\varepsilon = 0\) and if \(N < M\) or \(N>M\), the condition is not fulfilled. Therefore the model is, by ansatz, a quadratic term: $$ \epsilon_N = \epsilon_M + \Lambda (N - M)^2, \quad \Lambda > 0 $$ We then find the fraction volume of aggregates $$ X_N = N \left( \frac{X_M}{M} e^{\frac{M (\epsilon_M - \epsilon_N)}{k_B T}} \right)^{N/M} = N \left( \frac{X_M}{M} e^{-\frac{M \Lambda (N-M)^2}{k_B T}} \right)^{N/M} $$ This is a Gaussian distribution with a variance \(\sigma^2 = \langle|N-M|\rangle^2 = k_B T/2M\Lambda\) .

    ALSO, from the fact that $$ X_M = M \left( X_1 \, e^{\frac{\Delta \epsilon}{k_B T}} \right)^N $$ where \(\Delta \varepsilon = \varepsilon_1 - \varepsilon_1\) , the critical micelle concentration will be

    [!note] $\(\phi_c = \text{exp}(-\Delta\varepsilon/k_B T)\)$

    as, if \(X_1\) is below this amount, then the concentration is vanishingly small. Below this value the micelles start to form spontaneously when more molecules are added to the solution.
