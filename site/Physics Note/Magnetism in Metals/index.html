    Magnetism in Metals

    For noble metals and some alkali metals, they have delocalised electron in the free space. The magnetic response originates from the interaction between the spin of the electrons and the magnetic field.

    The energy of a single spin in magnetic field \(\mathbf{B}\) is $$E =\mu_s B = g_s \gamma \mathbf{S} \cdot \mathbf{B} = \mp \gamma \hbar B = \mp \mu_B B $$ The change in number of spin down electrons is (?) $$ \Delta n_{\downarrow} = -\frac{1}{2} g(E_F) \mu_B B $$ and it causes the increase of spin up electron by the same amount $$ \Delta n_{\uparrow} = -\Delta n_{\downarrow} = \frac{1}{2} g(E_F) \mu_B B $$

    The magnetisation is then $$ M = \mu_B (\Delta n_{\uparrow} - \Delta n_{\downarrow}) = g(E_F) \mu_B^2 B $$ $$\implies \chi = \frac{M}{H} \approx \frac{\mu_0 M}{B} = g(E_F) \mu_0 \mu_B^2 $$ The density of state is calculated using the same method as in Statistical Physics

    NOTE: the susceptibility is no longer temperature dependent.

    Stoner criterion

    However, it is not possible to keep moving the spin down electron to spin up. It has to balance the

    1. Increase in kinetic energy of transferred electrons
    2. Decrease in magnetic potential energy

    (i.e. the transfer of electron which increases kinetic energy causes the magnetic potential energy to decrease)

    Increase in kinetic energy

    Spin down electrons over energy range \(\delta E\) near \(E_F\) are transferred to spin up band. The increase in kinetic energy is then:

    \[ \Delta E_{KE} = \underbrace{\frac{1}{2} g(E_F) \delta E}_\text{number of electrons} \underbrace{\delta E}_\text{energy increase}\]

    Decrease in potential energy

    $$ \Delta E_{PE} = -\int_0^M \mathbf{B}\text{mf} \cdot d\mathbf{M} = -\int_0^M \lambda M' \, dM' = -\frac{\lambda M^2}{2} $$ Using the previous analysis on Pauli paramgnetism, $$ M = \mu_B (\Delta n) = g(E_F) \mu_B (\mu_B B) $$ by subs in the potential energy term we can find the expression for decrease in potential energy. } - \Delta n_{\downarrow

    For stable ferromagnetism where the potential is greater than kinetic, the spin down electrons are transferred to spin up because of the minimum energy configuration (the molecular field aligns with spin up). As they both balance, the molecular field stops the spin up to transfer to spin down. So the potential energy dominates: \(\Delta E<0\) (minimise energy)

    \[\implies Ug(E_F) > 1\]

    where \(U = \lambda\mu_B^2\) is the coulomb interaction. This condition means ferromagnetism is more likely in transition metals because \(g(E_F)\) is large due to a narrow energy \(d\) band.
