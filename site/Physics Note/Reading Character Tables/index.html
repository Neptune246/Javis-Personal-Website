    Reading Character Tables

    Group Theory

    Here we are going to use group theory in our character tables. The definition of group is that:

    1. Group is closed: \(\text{if} \ a\in V, b\in V, \text{then} ab\in V \text{and} a^2, b^2 \in V\)
    2. Commutative and existence of identity \(E\)
    3. Associative: \(A(BC) = (AB)C = ABC\)
    4. Reciprocal: \(1/a \in V, 1/b \in B\)
    5. Subgroup of a group is a subset of parent group that is also a group

    However, operations are not commutative i.e. the order matters.

    Character Tables

    • \(h\) is the order of group i.e. the sum of operations.
    • Irreps is irreducible representation i.e. the basis for the linear combination of reducible representation.
    • The right hand column \(z\), \(R_z\), \(x, R_y\) and \(y, R_x\) is the vectors that are affected by symmetry operations
    • The right-most column are the quadrupole
    • Characters (irreps) describe how basis functions are affected by operations

    Characters

    There are several ways to find characters i.e. reducible representation

    Inspection

    1. Pick the atoms or bonds as the basis for the operation
    2. Figure out the symmetry operations of the point group
    3. +1 if the basis is mapped onto itself; 0 if basis rotates into others; -1 if the vector is being reflected

    Example: Track the coordinate of each atoms in each transformation and you will get this: the factor of 3 is due to presence of 3 atoms.

    Traces of Transformation Matrices

    1. Find a basis (a bond)
    2. Identify the transformation matrix
    3. Find the trace

    Example: $$\begin{aligned}

    \text{Identity matrix} \quad I = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix} \

    \text{Rotation around \(z\) axis} \quad M = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}

    \end{aligned}$$

    Orbital

    When tracking orbital we can use the formula:

    • For a rotation by an angle \(\alpha\) about the \(z\) axis for an orbital with quantum number \(l\): $\(\chi^l_\alpha = \begin{cases} \frac{\sin\left[ \left( l + \frac{1}{2} \right) \alpha \right]}{\sin\left( \frac{\alpha}{2} \right)} & \text{for } \alpha \neq 0 \\ 2l + 1 & \text{for } \alpha = 0 \end{cases}\)$
    • For a roto-inversion operation: $\(\chi^l_{\bar{n}} = \chi^l(n \otimes \bar{1}) = (-1)^l \frac{\sin\left[ \left( l + 1/2 \right) \alpha \right]}{\sin\left( \frac{\alpha}{2} \right)}\)$

    Reducible Representation

    After finding the reducible representation such as \(\Gamma_z\) and \(\Gamma_x\) above, we want to express this in terms of linear combination of irreps on the character table to find the symmetrical axes and properties.

    We use the reduction formula in group theory: $\(n\Gamma_i = \frac{1}{h} \sum N_C \chi^\Gamma_C \chi^i_C\)$ where - \(h\) is the order of group (defined above), - \(N_C\) is the number of operation within the class (number beside the operation like 8 in \(8C_3\)) , - \(\chi^\Gamma_C\) and \(\chi_C^i\) are the characters in the reducible and irrep respectively.

    Application: Crystal Field Splitting for Transition Metal Ions

    Transition metal ions have their valence electrons partially occupying d orbitals which are strongly affected by their environment. This is because the orbitals extend sufficiently from the nucleus and the interaction with the wave functions of the surrounding species are significant.

    Many transition metal cations occupy octahedral environment i.e. \(O_h\) group. When we reduce the reducible representation, we find that \(\Gamma_{3d} = e_g + t_{2g}\) , meaning that the five 3d orbitals are no longer of the same symmetry and no longer degenerate in this octahedral site. They are, instead be described by triply-degenerate \(t_{2g}\) and doubly-degenerate \(e_g\) and these two sets of orbitals will have different energies.
