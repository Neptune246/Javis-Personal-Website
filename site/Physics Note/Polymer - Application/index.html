    Polymer Application

    Rubber

    We want to investigate the free energy when the rubber is stretched. We first consider that the incompressibility of rubber:

    $$ \lambda_x \lambda_y \lambda_z = 1 $$

    where \(\lambda_i\) is the stretching factor and stretching means the cross-link points upon deformation are$$ r_0^2 = x^2 + y^2 + z^2 \quad \mapsto \quad r_1^2 = \lambda_x^2 x^2 + \lambda_y^2 y^2 + \lambda_z^2 z^2 $$ From Polymer - Introduction, we know from random walk model that $$S = -k_B \frac{3r^2}{2Na^2} + \cancelto{0}{\text{constant}}= \Delta S_{\text{strand}} = -\frac{3k_B}{2Na^2} (r_1^2 - r_0^2) $$ by substituting the stretching factor into the entropy above, we get $$ \Delta S_{\text{volume}} = -\frac{nk_B}{2} (\lambda_x^2 + \lambda_y^2 + \lambda_z^2 - 3) $$ where \(n\) is the number of polymers per unit volume. Assuming the deformation is along the \(x\) direction, using the incompressibility identity, we get $$ \Delta G = T \Delta S_{\text{volume}} = -\frac{n k_B T}{2} \left( \lambda^2 + \frac{2}{\lambda} - 3 \right) $$ where we assume \(H =0\) at this moment as the interactions can be assumed unchanged.

    Tensile stress

    The tensile stress is then $$ \sigma_T = -\frac{\partial G_T}{\partial e_T} = n k_B \left[ 1 + e - \frac{1}{(1 + e)^2} \right] $$ where \(\lambda = 1 + e\) . From here we can find Young modulus for small \(e\) $$ E = 3nk_B T $$

    and the shear modulus $$ G = nk_B T = \frac{\rho RT}{M} $$

    Viscoelasticity in Polymers

    We can also find the viscoelasticity in polymer, given that in [[Physics/Setup/Introduction to Soft Condensed Matter Physics|Introduction to Soft Condensed Matter Physics]] we mentioned that everything is viscoelastic in a given timescale.

    To find the evolution of stress after a given strain, \(e\), we can define a stress relaxation function, \(G(t)\) $\(\sigma(t) = e_0 G(t)\)$

    and to find the evolution of strain after a given stress \(\sigma\), we can define a strain relaxation function, \(J(t)\) $\(e(t) = \sigma_0J(t)\)$

    and they (stress example in this case) can be generalised to

    [!note] $$ \sigma(t) = \int_{t'=t_0}^t G(t-t') \, d[e(t')] = \int_{t_0}^t G(t-t') \dot{\epsilon}(t') \, dt' $$

    where we are looking for a small change in \(e(t)\) with respect to time and the stress or strain while the stress relaxation function is the same.

    From there, we can define the complex modulus

    [!Definition] $\(G^*(\omega) = i\omega \int_0^\infty e^{-i\omega s} \, G(s) \, ds = \underbrace{G'(\omega)}_{\substack{\text{storage modulus:} \\ \text{elastic behaviour} \\ \text{of the system}}} + i \underbrace{G''(\omega)}_{\substack{\text{loss modulus:} \\ \text{viscous behaviour} \\ \text{of the system}}}\)$

    where \(\omega = 2\pi/\tau\) is the shearing/stressing frequency corresponding to the relaxation timescale \(\tau\) .

    For a system relaxing exponentially, $\(G(t) \propto e^{-t/\tau}\)$ is the Maxwell's model

    Experimental Investigation of Relaxation Function with respect to time

    This is a graph for linear polymers where the distribution of relative molecular mass is narrow.

    There are 3 stages:

    1. Early relaxation which does not depend on the degree of polymerisation
    2. Plateau stage where the polymer behave like an elastic solid and it does not depend on the degree of polymerisation
    3. Late relaxation which depends on the degree of polymerisation where plateau ends at the terminal, relaxation timescale \(\tau\) and has a relationship $\(\tau \sim N^{3.4}\)$ At this stage, the polymer behaves like viscous liquid

    In fact the plateau can be explained by the entanglement and reptation model. Entanglement model originates from the fact that the polymers cannot intersect each other when stretched so local entanglement points will be created temporarily. But when it comes to disentanglement, we have to use reptation model which is not trivial.
