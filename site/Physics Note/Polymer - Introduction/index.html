    Polymer Introduction

    • Polymer is a giant molecule made up of many repeating units covalently joined together in the form of a long chain

    • Homopolymer: Polymer composed of only one type of molecule

    • Copolymer: Two more more repeat units

    • Block copolymer: repeating units are arranged in blocks

    • Degree of polymerisation: number of repeat units present in a chain

    At a given temperature, polymeric materials are in one of the following states:

    • Liquid
    • Glass
    • Crystalline
    • Liquid Crystalline: Kevlar

    Dimension of Polymer Chains

    We are using random walk model to describe the relationship between degree of polymerisation \(N\) and the overall dimensions of the polymer chain.

    Assuming each link \(i\) is defined by a vector \(\vec{a}_i\) and the polymer end-to-end vector \(\vec{r}\) is $$ \vec{r} = \vec{a_1} + \vec{a_2} + \cdots + \vec{a_N} = \sum_{i=1}^N \vec{a_i} $$ $\(\implies \langle \vec{r} \cdot \vec{r} \rangle = \left( \sum_{i=1}^N \vec{a_i} \right) \cdot \left( \sum_{j=1}^N \vec{a_j} \right) = N a^2 + \underbrace{\left( \sum_{i \neq j} \vec{a_i} \cdot \vec{a_j} \right)}_{= 0 \text{ (random orient.)}}\)$

    In fact, the summation term is finite and negligible in real polymer chains. See lecture note.

    Using this along with the probability distribution of end-to-end distances $$ P(N, r) = \left( \frac{2\pi N a^2}{3} \right)^{-3/2} e^{-\frac{3r^2}{2Na^2}} $$ and Boltzmann entropy $$ S(r) = k_B \ln P(N, r) = -k_B \frac{3r^2}{2Na^2} + \text{constant} $$

    We get $\(G_{el}(r) = \cancel{H(r)} - T S(r) = k_B T \frac{3r^2}{2Na^2} + \text{constant}\)$ where \(H(r) =0\) because enthalpic contribution is negligible (we will include this later) as 1. freely jointed chain model does not take into account any molecular interactions
    2. the polymer with multiple segments are able to occupy the same space

    Revision of Random Walk Model (Flory's model)

    Theoretical value

    We, so far, do not care about the volume occupied by the polymer. The chain can actually overlap itself $$ R = \sqrt{\langle \vec{r}^2 \rangle} = a\sqrt{N} $$ with a density of $$ \rho \sim \frac{N}{R^3} \sim \frac{1}{\sqrt{N}} = N^{-1/2} $$

    Real life

    In reality, the polymer cannot intersect themselves. So 'self-avoiding walk' model should be used.

    The derivation of free energy of Flory model is far from trivial. We will quote the results and assumptions:

    • Each monomer occupies a small volume \(v\sim a^3\)
    • The concentration of the segments is \(c \sim N/r^3\)

    The Gibb's free energy is $$ G_{\text{excl}}(r) = -T S_{\text{excl}}(r) = k_B T v \frac{N^2}{2r^3} > 0 $$ where \(S = k_Bv N/V\)

    Combining this with the random walk model gives the total expression for the Gibbs free energy:

    $$ G(r) = k_B T \frac{3r^2}{2Na^2} + k_B T v \frac{N^2}{2r^3} + \text{cte} $$

    Therefore the equilibrium end-to-end distance is, in practice, $$ \sqrt{\langle \vec{r}^2 \rangle} = aN^\nu $$ where \(\nu = 3/5\).

    Second correction to random-walk model (Molecular interactions and interfacial energy)

    In random walk model, we mentioned that the enthalpic contribution is zero but it is not correct in reality. We have to consider:

    1. polymer-polymer contact
    2. polymer-solvent contact
    3. solvent-solvent contact

    Polymer-polymer contact

    $$ N_{pp} = \frac{1}{2} z N v c $$ with
    - \(z\) the number of nearest monomer neighbours
    - \(c\) the concentration of polymer segments
    - \(v\) is the volume of a monomer so \(v c\) is the probability of seeing another monomer along the chain

    Polymer-solvent contact

    $$ N_{ps} = z N v (\rho_s - c) $$ with
    - \(\rho_s\) is the concentration of solvent molecules in the bulk liquid

    Solvent-solvent contact

    $$ N_{ss} = N_{ss}^0 - N_{ps} - N_{pp} $$ where
    - \(N_{ss}^0\) is the number of solvent-solvent contacts in the absence of the polymer

    The total enthalpy of the system is then
    $$ U_{\text{int}} = N_{pp} \epsilon_{pp} + N_{ps} \epsilon_{ps} + N_{ss} \epsilon_{ss} $$ $$ \implies \frac{1}{2} z N v c (\epsilon_{pp} + \epsilon_{ss} - 2 \epsilon_{ps}) + \underbrace{z N v (\epsilon_{ps} - \epsilon_{ss}) + N_{ss}^0 \epsilon_{ss}}_{cte} $$

    $$ \implies U_{\text{int}} = -2 \chi v \frac{N^2}{2 r^3} + \text{constant} = H < 0$$ where \(c \sim N/r^3\) and \(\chi = zNvc(\varepsilon_{ps}-\varepsilon_{pp}-\varepsilon_{ss})/2\) and the pressure is constant.

    Combining the enthalpy and entropy from Flory model derived above, we have

    [!note] $$ G = H - TS = \underbrace{k_B T v \left( 1 - \frac{2 \chi}{k_B T} \right) \frac{N^2}{2 r^3}}{G}} + \underbrace{\frac{3 k_B T}{2 N a^2} r^2{G $$}} + \text{constant

    We can distinguish the behaviour of the polymer solution - If \(\chi < k_B T/2\) , it becomes Flory model as polymer extended in solvent good solvent (because \(G_{excl} > H\) so entropy increases) - If \(\chi = k_B T/2\), \(G_{excl}\) cancels with \(H \implies\) theta condition where all corrections can be ignored - If \(\chi < k_B T/2\), \(H > G_{excl}\), the interfacial energy between the polymer and solvent dominates, hence poor solvent
