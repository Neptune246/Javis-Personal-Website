    Properties of free electron model (continuous)

    Pauli magnetic susceptibility \(\chi = M/H = \mu_0M/B\)

    The energy of the electrons will be perturbed by the magnetic field: $\(E = \pm \mu_B B\)$

    Transport (shift of Fermi sphere)

    Recall from here that \(J = \sigma E\) , \(\sigma = 1/\rho\) and \(F = \Delta p/\Delta t = \hbar dk/dt = -|e|E\), we get $\(\hbar dk = -|e|Edt\)$This means that we shift the Fermi sphere by a little bit when an electric field is applied to the Fermi sphere. Due to the impurities and phonons that cause scattering, there is a tendency of Fermi sphere to go back to equilibrium by randomising electron momenta.

    The mean free path is \(\lambda \approx V_F \tau\)

    Recall also that \(J = -n|e|V_d\) here and by substituting \(V_d = -|e|E\tau/m\) (get from \(\Delta p = mV_d\)), we recognise that $\(J = \sigma E = \frac{n|e|^2\tau}{m}E\)$ This step shows that the electric field creates a net momentum for electron (first step) and that net momentum can be converted to drift velocity and from the formula \(J = -n|e|V_d\), we know that the drift velocity can can cause the metal to be conductive. Hence the shift in Fermi sphere can be used to explain why metals are conductive.

    Metals have a partially filled Fermi sphere, so a small shift creates current. Insulators/semiconductors have a fully filled valence band, so the Fermi sphere cannot shift meaningfully. (But how exactly?)

    However, Drude has no explaination of electron scattering. It assumes that the scattering time is constant. However, the scattering time depends on electron energy and is caused by phonons and impurities.

    Hall effect

    \(R_H\) is Hall coefficient

    In Drude model, \(R_H = - 1/ne\) but in reality, some metals like Al and Be have positive \(R_H\) due to hole-like curvature.
