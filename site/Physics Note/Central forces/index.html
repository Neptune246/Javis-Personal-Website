    Central forces

    Two interacting bodies

    Central forces are the forces that only depend on the distance \(r\) between two bodies, directed along the line between them. The general case of 2 point masses has a Lagrangian of $$ L = \frac{1}{2} (m_1 \dot{r}1^2 + m_2 \dot{r}_2^2) - \underbrace{V_1(r_1) - V_2(r_2)} $$ and if there is no external potentials, then the Lagrangian is translationally invariant i.e. }} - \underbrace{V_{12}(|r_1 - r_2|)}_{\text{interaction potential}\(L(r_1, r_2, \dot{r}_1, \dot{r}_2) = L(r_1 + a, r_2 + a, \dot{r}_1, \dot{r}_2)\)

    Ignorable CoM Motion and conservation of momentum

    The Lagrangian above can be rewritten in terms of \(\underline{R}\), \(R = \frac{m_1 r_1 + m_2 r_2}{m_1 + m_2}\) and the relative coordinate \(\underline{r} = \underline{r_1} - \underline{r_2}\) $$ L = \frac{1}{2} M \dot{\underline{R}}^2 + \frac{1}{2} \mu \dot{r}^2 - V_{12}(r) $$ where \(M = m_1+m_2\) and \(\mu = m_1m_2/(m_1 +m_2)\). \(R\) is an ignorable coordinate as it does not explicitly appear in the Lagrangian.

    If we switch to CoM frame (a frame moving with CoM), the \(\underline{R} = \underline{\dot{R}} = 0\) and thus, \(L = (1/2)\mu \underline{\dot{r}}^2 - V_{12}(r)\) (rotational invariant)

    By using spherical coordinates $$ L = \frac{1}{2} \mu \left[ \dot{r}^2 + r^2 \dot{\theta}^2 + r^2 \dot{\phi}^2 \sin^2 \theta \right] - V_{12}(r) $$ where the angular momentum in \(z\) direction is $$ p_{\phi} = \frac{\partial L}{\partial \dot{\phi}} = \mu r^2 \dot{\phi} \sin^2 \theta = \text{constant} \equiv J_z $$

    For any choice of z axis, the angular momentum in that direction is constant. Without loss of generality, we can choose the coordinates such that \(J\) always lies along the z axis

    Equivalent 1D problem

    We wish to remove the \(\theta\) and \(\dot{\phi}\) term to simplify the calculation. Following the Lagrangian in the spherical coordinates, we substitute \(\dot{\theta} = 0\), \(\theta = \pi/2\), \(\dot{\phi} = J/\mu r^2\) (because \(J= rp =\mu r^2\dot{\phi}\)), we get the total energy is$$ E = \frac{1}{2} \mu \dot{r}^2 + \underbrace{\frac{J^2}{2\mu r^2} + V_{12}(r)}{V $$ The effective potential is the combination of the angular momentum barrier (centrifugal force since it arises from the choice of reference frame, not other physics) and interaction potential. The Euler-Lagrange equation yields $$ \mu \ddot{r} = \frac{J^2}{\mu r^3} - \frac{\partial V_{12}}{\partial r} $$ which will be used }}(r)in the next chapter
