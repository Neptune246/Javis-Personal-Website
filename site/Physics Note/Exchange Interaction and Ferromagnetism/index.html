    Exchange Interaction and Ferromagnetism

    So far we have considered a Hamtiltonian that deals only with paramagnetic and diamagnetic terms, in fact we should also consider the interaction between atoms in a multi-electron systems.

    The medium between interaction can be: 1. Superexchange in ionic solids, e.g. MnO neighbouring \(\text{Mn}^{2+}\) interact via \(\text{O}^{2-}\) 2. RKKY interaction: alignment of spins in ferromagnetic metals via conduction electrons

    Weiss model of Ferromagnetism

    \[ -J_{ij} \mathbf{S}_i \cdot \mathbf{S}_j = \begin{cases} \dfrac{3J_{ij}\hbar^2}{4} & (\text{singlet}) \\[10pt] -\dfrac{J_{ij}\hbar^2}{4} & (\text{triplet}) \end{cases} \]

    To simplify the calculation and explanation of ferromagnetism, we define a fictitious field called molecular field, \(\mathbf{B}_{\text{mf}}\) $$ \mathbf{B}{\text{mf}} = \frac{1}{\gamma g_J} \sum} -J_{ij} \mathbf{Sj = \lambda \mathbf{M} $$ and it is the magnetic field formed by the magnetic field from the magnetic moment. Assuming identical atoms/ions with \(\mathbf{L} =0\) and \(\mathbf{S} = \mathbf{J}\), we have $$ \hat{H} $$}} = \sum_i \gamma g_J (\mathbf{B} + \mathbf{B}_{\text{mf}}) \cdot \mathbf{J

    This form is similar to the one with Paramagnetism and from there, remember that $$ \frac{M}{M_s} = B_J(y) \quad ; \quad B_J(y) = \frac{2J + 1}{2J} \coth\left(\frac{2J + 1}{2J}y\right) - \frac{1}{2J} \coth\left(\frac{y}{2J}\right) $$ Here, since we just introduce the molecular field, we simply include \(\lambda M\) term into the solution \(y\) $$ y = \frac{g_J \mu_B J ( \cancel{B} + B_{\text{mf}})}{k_B T} = \frac{g_J \mu_B J ( \cancel{B} + \lambda M)}{k_B T} $$ where we assume \(B\) is neglegible. If we rearrange this to find the relationship between \(M\) and \(y\), we get a straight line which represent different temperature.

    This solution suggests that as the temperature decreases until it reaches a certain critical temperature (Curie temperature), the ferromagnets will have a spontaneous magnetisation. (Phase Transition). This is due to the presence of the molecular field.

    The result is similar to Paramagnetism except there is an additional term: $$ \frac{M}{M_s} = \frac{\mu_B (B + \lambda M)}{k_B T} $$ $$ \implies \chi = \frac{\mu_0 \mu_B M_s}{k_B \left[ T - \frac{\lambda \mu_B M_s}{k_B} \right]} \propto \frac{1}{T - T_C} $$

    for small magnetisation \(\chi = \mu_0M/B\)

    Helimagnetism

    The exchange energy is $\(E = -2NS^2(J_1\cos\theta+J_2\cos2\theta)\)$ where \(N\) is the number of atoms in the plane. The minimum energy condition is \(dE/d\theta = 0\) $\((J_1+4J_2\cos\theta)\sin\theta = 0\)$

    \[ \sin \theta = 0 \begin{cases} \theta = 0^\circ \text{ (ferromagnetic)} \\ \theta = 180^\circ \text{ (antiferromagnetic)} \end{cases} \]
    \[ \cos \theta = -\frac{J_1}{4J_2} \text{ (helimagnetism)} \]
